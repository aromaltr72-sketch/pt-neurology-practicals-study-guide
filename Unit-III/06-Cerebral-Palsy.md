# Unit III: Cerebral Palsy - Comprehensive Study Guide

## 1. DEFINITION & PATHOPHYSIOLOGY

### Definition
Cerebral Palsy (CP) is a group of permanent disorders of movement and posture caused by nonprogressive disturbances in the developing brain, most often before, during, or shortly after birth. It is characterized by abnormal motor control due to brain damage affecting motor areas.

### Classification by Type (Motor Pattern)

**Spastic Cerebral Palsy (75-80%)**
- Hypertonicity (increased muscle tone)
- Hyper-reflexia (brisk reflexes)
- Positive Babinski sign
- Clasp-knife resistance to passive movement
- Results from upper motor neuron lesion

**Dyskinetic/Athetoid Cerebral Palsy (15-20%)**
- Involuntary, uncontrolled movements
- Difficulty controlling muscle tone
- Hypotonia or fluctuating tone
- Movement increases with emotion/stress
- Choreoathetoid movements

**Ataxic Cerebral Palsy (5-10%)**
- Incoordination, poor balance
- Hypotonia
- Intention tremor
- Dysmetria
- Poor spatial orientation

**Mixed Type**
- Combination of above features

### Distribution Classification

**Monoplegia:** One limb affected
**Hemiplegia:** One side of body affected (most common, 40-50%)
**Diplegia:** Both lower extremities primarily affected (30-35%)
**Triplegia:** Three limbs affected
**Quadriplegia:** All four limbs affected (most severe, 10-15%)

## 2. ETIOLOGY & RISK FACTORS

### Prenatal Factors (60-70% of cases)
- Intrauterine infection (TORCH: Toxoplasmosis, Rubella, CMV, Herpes)
- Maternal infections
- Placental abnormalities
- Genetic factors
- Intrauterine stroke
- Maternal metabolic disorders

### Perinatal Factors (20-30% of cases)
- Birth asphyxia/hypoxic-ischemic encephalopathy (HIE)
- Prematurity and associated complications
- Intraventricular hemorrhage
- Kernicterus (from severe jaundice)
- Birth trauma
- Low birth weight

### Postnatal Factors (10% of cases)
- Traumatic brain injury
- Infections (meningitis, encephalitis)
- Hypoxic episodes
- Stroke
- Nonaccidental injury

## 3. CLINICAL FEATURES & PRESENTATION

### Early Signs (Infancy)
- Delayed motor milestones (not reaching milestones on time)
- Abnormal muscle tone (too stiff or too floppy)
- Asymmetrical movements
- Persistent primitive reflexes beyond expected age
- Poor head control
- Difficulty with feeding
- Excessive drooling

### Motor Features by Type

**Spastic CP:**
- Increased tone (hypertonicity)
- Hyper-reflexia
- Positive Babinski sign
- Clasp-knife spasticity
- Characteristic posturing:
  - Flexed arms, extended legs (in hemiplegia)
  - Scissor walking (in diplegia)
  - "Equinus" position (toes pointed)

**Dyskinetic CP:**
- Uncontrolled movements (choreoathetosis)
- Fluctuating tone
- Worse with emotion, fatigue, stress
- Difficulty maintaining posture
- May have orofacial involvement (dysarthria, drooling)

**Ataxic CP:**
- Poor balance and coordination
- Hypotonia (low tone)
- Wide-based gait
- Intention tremor
- Dysmetria (inaccuracy of movement)

### Associated Features

**Orthopedic Complications**
- Contractures (especially ankle plantarflexors, hip flexors)
- Scoliosis (common in spastic quadriplegia)
- Hip dislocation (spastic quadriplegia)
- Limb length discrepancies
- Deformities

**Cognitive & Developmental**
- Intellectual disability (30-40%)
- Learning disabilities
- Speech and language impairment
- Visual impairment (strabismus, refractive errors)
- Hearing impairment

**Seizure Disorder**
- 30-40% of CP patients
- Higher rate with quadriplegia

**Pain**
- Chronic pain (musculoskeletal)
- From muscle spasticity and contractures
- Important to assess and manage

**Gastrointestinal Issues**
- Difficulty swallowing (dysphagia)
- Gastroesophageal reflux
- Constipation
- Aspiration risk

## 4. ASSESSMENT PROCEDURES & CLINICAL TESTS

### Gross Motor Assessment

**Gross Motor Function Classification System (GMFCS)**
- Levels I-V
- Level I: Walks without limitations
- Level V: Cannot walk, significant physical assistance needed
- Highly predictive of outcomes
- Useful for functional classification

**Gross Motor Function Measure (GFMM)**
- 88-item assessment
- Motor skills across: lying/rolling, sitting, crawling, kneeling, standing, walking, running, jumping
- Standardized scoring (0-3 scale)
- Excellent psychometric properties
- Gold standard for motor assessment in CP

**Timed & Distance Tests**
- 6-Minute Walk Test (if ambulatory)
- 10-Meter Walk Test
- Timed Up and Go
- Berg Balance Scale

### Tone Assessment

**Modified Ashworth Scale**
- Assesses spasticity (0-4+ scale)
- Document tone in major muscle groups
- Useful for tracking changes
- Baseline and follow-up

### Functional Assessment

**Functional Independence Measure (FIM)**
- 18 items
- Assesses ADL independence
- 1-7 scale (1 = total assist, 7 = complete independence)

**Pediatric Functional Independence Measure (WeeFIM)**
- For children
- Adapted FIM items
- Developmental appropriateness

### Range of Motion & Contracture Assessment

**Passive ROM (PROM) Measurement**
- Goniometric assessment
- Key areas:
  - Ankle plantarflexion (common contracture)
  - Hip flexion
  - Hip adduction
  - Knee flexion
  - Elbow flexion
- Compare side-to-side
- Track progression

**Modified Tardieu Scale**
- More sensitive for spasticity assessment
- Measures angle of catch
- Distinguishes true contracture from spasticity

### Manual Muscle Testing (MMT)
- Standard 0-5 scale
- Affected vs non-affected limbs
- Note distribution pattern
- Asymmetry common in hemiplegia

### Neurological Examination

**Reflex Assessment**
- Deep tendon reflexes (hyperactive in spastic CP)
- Primitive reflex persistence (abnormal if persists)
- Babinski sign (positive in spastic CP)

**Coordination Assessment** (Ataxic CP)
- Finger-to-nose test
- Heel-shin test
- Rapid alternating movements
- Dysdiadochokinesia assessment

**Gait Analysis**
- Visual assessment of gait pattern
- Common patterns:
  - Scissor gait (hemiplegia/diplegia)
  - Equinus gait (toe-walking)
  - Asymmetric gait (hemiplegia)
  - Ataxic gait (ataxic CP)
- Quantified (3D gait analysis if available)

## 5. MANAGEMENT PROTOCOLS

### A. Medical Management (Overview)

**Spasticity Management**
- Botulinum toxin injections (focal muscle groups)
- Oral medications: baclofen, tizanidine
- Intrathecal baclofen (severe spasticity)
- Serial casting

**Seizure Management**
- Antiepileptic drugs if seizures present

**Orthopedic Surgery**
- Tendon lengthening
- Transfer procedures
- Hip surgery (if dislocation risk)
- Spinal fusion (if scoliosis severe)

### B. Physical Therapy Management

**Goals**
1. Maximize motor function and independence
2. Prevent/delay contractures and deformities
3. Optimize gait and mobility
4. Enhance functional skills
5. Prevent secondary complications
6. Support development (in children)
7. Prepare for transitions (school, adulthood)

**Principles**
- Early intervention is crucial (neuroplasticity highest in young children)
- High-intensity, task-specific practice
- Functional, meaningful activities
- Family-centered approach
- Long-term management (lifelong)
- Interdisciplinary coordination

**Interventions**

**1. Stretching & Contracture Prevention**
- Daily stretching program
- Static stretching (30-60 seconds)
- 5-10 repetitions per muscle group
- Key muscles: ankle plantarflexors, hip flexors, hamstrings
- Nighttime splinting (orthoses)
- Positioning programs
- Important: Early prevention easier than treating fixed contractures

**2. Strengthening Program**
- Resistance exercises (appropriate to ability)
- Focus on:
  - Antigravity muscles
  - Weak muscle groups
  - Functional movement patterns
- Avoid overfatigue
- Progressive resistance
- Task-specific strengthening

**3. Spasticity Management**
- Stretching (as above)
- Heat application before stretching
- Tone-reducing techniques:
  - Slow, rhythmic movements
  - Reciprocal inhibition
  - Weight-bearing (sometimes reduces tone)
- Serial splinting/casting
- Coordination with medical management

**4. Motor Retraining & Functional Skills**

**For Ambulatory Children:**
- Gait training
- Address gait deviations (scissor gait, equinus walking)
- Balance training
- Stair climbing
- Community ambulation
- Orthotic fitting and training (AFO, SMO)
- Fall prevention

**For Non-Ambulatory Children:**
- Wheelchair mobility
- Transfers (bed, chair, toilet)
- Powered mobility if appropriate
- Community access training

**5. Balance & Coordination Training**
- Static balance progression
- Dynamic balance activities
- Perturbation training
- Functional reaching
- Dual-task training
- Agility exercises

**6. Aquatic Therapy**
- Benefits:
  - Gravity reduction (easier movement)
  - Resistance without impact
  - Muscle relaxation in warm water
  - Psychological benefits
- Excellent modality for CP
- Can achieve higher repetitions

**7. Constraint-Induced Movement Therapy (CIMT)**
- For hemiplegia
- Constraint of less-affected arm
- Intensive practice of affected arm
- Evidence supports effectiveness
- Promotes motor learning and neuroplasticity

**8. Robot-Assisted Therapy**
- Emerging evidence for gait training
- Body weight-supported treadmill training
- Intensive repetitive practice
- May improve gait speed and endurance

**9. Functional Electrical Stimulation (FES)**
- Stimulates weak muscles
- Can supplement strengthening
- Particularly for dorsiflexors (foot drop)
- Combined with PT for better results

**10. Orthotic Prescription & Management**
- AFO (ankle-foot orthosis): Most common
  - Corrects foot drop (dorsiflexor weakness)
  - Prevents plantarflexion contracture
  - Improves gait pattern
  - Improves safety
- SMO (supramalleolar orthosis)
- KAFO (knee-ankle-foot orthosis): For knee weakness
- Dynamic orthoses (emerging technology)
- Proper fitting and adjustment critical
- Training on use and care

**11. Mobility Training**
- Bed mobility
- Transfers (depends on classification/severity)
- Wheelchair propulsion (if applicable)
- Community mobility
- Fall prevention strategies

**12. ADL Training**
- Feeding
- Dressing
- Hygiene
- Adapted techniques
- Compensatory strategies
- Assistive devices
- Environmental modification

**13. Developmental Facilitation** (Especially young children)
- Promote normal movement patterns
- Inhibit abnormal reflexes (in young children)
- Facilitate normal postural reactions
- Support age-appropriate development

**14. Family Education & Home Program**
- Parent training crucial
- Home exercise program
- Positioning strategies
- Handling techniques
- Activity suggestions
- Resources and support
- Advocacy support

**15. Psychosocial Support**
- Assist in adjustment to diagnosis
- Promote self-confidence
- Address depression/anxiety
- Peer support groups
- Transition planning (for adolescents)
- Vocational assessment
- Quality of life optimization

## 6. PT EVALUATION POINTS FOR PRACTICALS

### Assessment Components
- Determine type (spastic, dyskinetic, ataxic)
- Determine distribution (hemiplegia, diplegia, quadriplegia)
- Classify using GMFCS
- Perform GFMM (if trained)
- Assess tone using Modified Ashworth
- Measure ROM/contractures
- Evaluate gait (if ambulatory)
- Functional assessment (FIM or similar)
- Identify secondary complications

### Treatment Planning
- Age-appropriate goal setting
- Maximize functional independence
- Prevent contractures (critical in young child)
- Address identified impairments
- Orthotic prescription if indicated
- Family/caregiver education
- Long-term management plan

## 7. KEY POINTS TO REMEMBER

✓ **Non-progressive brain damage** - but motor skills can improve with therapy
✓ **GMFCS useful** - predicts ambulatory potential
✓ **Early intervention crucial** - neuroplasticity highest in young children
✓ **Contracture prevention essential** - easier than treating established contractures
✓ **Spastic type most common** - 75-80% of cases
✓ **Hemiplegia most common distribution** - affects one side
✓ **Family-centered approach** - parents are primary interventionists
✓ **High-intensity practice effective** - more repetitions = better outcomes
✓ **Functional training priority** - meaningful, relevant activities
✓ **Long-term management** - continues into adulthood
✓ **Multidisciplinary coordination** - PT, OT, speech, orthotics, medicine
✓ **Prognosis variable** - depends on severity and distribution
