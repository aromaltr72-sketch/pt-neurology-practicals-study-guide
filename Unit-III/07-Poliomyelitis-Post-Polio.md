# Unit III: Poliomyelitis & Post-Polio Syndrome - Comprehensive Study Guide

## 1. POLIOMYELITIS - DEFINITION & PATHOPHYSIOLOGY

### Definition
Poliomyelitis is an acute viral infection caused by the poliovirus that primarily affects the anterior horn cells (motor neurons) of the spinal cord, resulting in paralysis. It is now rare in most of the world due to vaccination programs, but historically was a major cause of disability.

### Pathophysiology

**Viral Infection**
- Poliovirus enters through gastrointestinal tract
- Replicates in lymphoid tissue and intestine
- Viremia (virus in bloodstream)
- Crosses blood-brain barrier
- Infects anterior horn cells (lower motor neurons)

**Neuronal Damage**
- Inflammation and destruction of motor neurons
- Results in denervation of muscles
- Flaccid paralysis
- Typically asymmetric pattern
- Respiratory muscles may be affected (severe cases)

## 2. CLINICAL FEATURES

### Presentation Types

**Abortive Poliomyelitis (70-75%)**
- Mild, flu-like symptoms
- Fever, malaise, headache
- No paralysis
- Complete recovery

**Non-Paralytic Poliomyelitis (24-25%)**
- More severe systemic symptoms
- Meningeal signs (stiff neck, sensitivity to light)
- No paralysis
- Full recovery

**Paralytic Poliomyelitis (0.1-0.5%)**
- Biphasic course:
  - Initial febrile period (fever, malaise)
  - Apparent recovery
  - Then sudden onset of paralysis
- **Asymmetric flaccid paralysis** (key feature)
- Lower extremities more commonly affected than upper
- Can involve respiratory muscles (bulbar polio)
- Areflexia (absent reflexes)
- Sensory changes absent
- Progression typically over 3-5 days, then stabilizes

### Motor Patterns

**Spinal Poliomyelitis**
- Asymmetric limb paralysis
- Lower extremities usually more affected
- Can progress to quadriplegia
- Respiratory involvement possible (diaphragm)

**Bulbar Poliomyelitis**
- Cranial nerve involvement (CN IX, X, XII)
- Difficulty swallowing
- Slurred speech
- Weak palate
- Respiratory compromise (airway muscles affected)
- Most severe form
- High mortality if respiratory muscles involved

---

## 3. HISTORICAL PT MANAGEMENT (Acute Phase)

**Iron Lungs**
- Used for respiratory muscle paralysis
- Negative pressure ventilation
- Allowed survival of severe cases
- PT focused on positioning, ROM

**Rehabilitation**
- Positioning to prevent contractures
- ROM exercises
- Gradual mobilization as recovery occurred
- Orthotic prescription
- Gait retraining
- Many recovered significant function
- Some left with residual paralysis

---

## 4. POST-POLIO SYNDROME (PPS)

### Definition
Post-Polio Syndrome is a constellation of new symptoms occurring 15-40+ years after the acute poliomyelitis infection in individuals who had paralytic polio and made a good functional recovery.

### Pathophysiology

**Motor Unit Remodeling**
- Following polio, surviving motor neurons sprout to reinnervate denervated muscle fibers
- Enlarged motor units (more fibers per neuron)
- Over decades: slow deterioration of these enlarged motor units
- Loss of motor unit stability
- Gradual denervation despite recovery phase

**Proposed Mechanisms**
- Motor neuron loss from enlarged motor units
- Synaptic dysfunction
- Loss of myelin
- Muscle fiber degeneration
- Inflammatory processes
- Weakness in initially unaffected muscles

---

## 5. POST-POLIO SYNDROME - CLINICAL FEATURES

### New Symptoms (After stable recovery period)

**Muscle Weakness & Fatigue**
- Progressive weakness in muscles affected by original polio
- New weakness in previously unaffected limbs (frequent)
- Disproportionate fatigue (distinguishing feature)
- Fatigue worse with:
  - Exertion
  - Heat
  - Stress
  - Evening hours
  - Spring and fall seasons

**Pain**
- Joint pain (from overuse, biomechanical changes)
- Muscle pain (myalgias)
- Nerve pain (neuropathic)
- Often localized to previously affected muscles

**Functional Decline**
- Loss of independence in ADLs
- Difficulty with previously tolerated activities
- Progressive limitation
- May return to wheelchair use after years of walking

**Other Features**
- Cold intolerance (affected limbs)
- Muscle atrophy/wasting
- Breathing difficulties (if bulbar involvement)
- Swallowing difficulties (if bulbar involvement)
- Sleep disturbances
- Depression and anxiety

### Diagnostic Criteria for PPS

**Required:**
1. Confirmed history of paralytic poliomyelitis
2. Partial or full recovery followed by 15+ years of stable function
3. NEW onset of progressive weakness in:
   - Previously affected muscles
   - OR previously unaffected muscles (frequent)
4. Duration: ≥12 months

**Supportive (but not required):**
- Fatigue (disproportionate)
- Joint/muscle pain
- Muscle atrophy
- Electromyography: neurogenic pattern, motor unit instability

## 6. POST-POLIO ASSESSMENT & MANAGEMENT

### Assessment

**Manual Muscle Testing**
- Baseline strength documentation
- Compare to prior testing (if available)
- Identify pattern of weakness
- Quantify decline

**Functional Assessment**
- Activities that have become difficult
- Baseline: ADL independence
- Gait changes
- Endurance changes
- Environmental assessment

**Pain & Fatigue Assessment**
- Pain scale and location
- Fatigue severity
- Triggers for fatigue
- Impact on function

**Respiratory Assessment** (If bulbar involvement)
- Vital capacity
- Dyspnea on exertion
- Sleep apnea assessment
- Swallowing assessment

### Physical Therapy Management

**Goals**
1. Maintain function and independence
2. Manage pain and fatigue
3. Prevent further deterioration
4. Optimize energy conservation
5. Adapt to changing abilities

**Key Principles**
- **Avoid overuse** (critical in PPS)
- Activity pacing
- Energy conservation
- Gentle strengthening (not aggressive)
- Low-impact activities
- Frequent rest periods

**Interventions**

**1. Energy Conservation & Activity Pacing**
- Identify activities causing excessive fatigue
- Modify activities to reduce energy demands
- Schedule activities with rest periods
- Prioritize important activities
- Alternate between easy and moderate tasks
- Avoid "no pain, no gain" approach
- Critical: Overuse can accelerate decline

**2. Gentle Strengthening**
- Low-intensity, low-repetition exercises
- NOT high-intensity resistance
- Submaximal effort (50-75% of maximum)
- Frequency: 3 days/week with rest days
- Duration: Shorter sessions
- Modalities:
  - Isometric exercises
  - Gentle active ROM
  - Aquatic exercise (excellent - support without overload)
  - Tai chi
  - Yoga (modified)
- Monitor for overuse symptoms

**3. Pain Management**
- Gentle stretching (not aggressive)
- Heat application
- Manual therapy (gentle)
- Postural optimization
- Activity modification
- Ergonomic assessment
- Stress reduction
- Sleep optimization

**4. Fatigue Management**
- Lifestyle modifications
- Sleep hygiene optimization
- Temperature control (avoid heat)
- Stress management
- Pacing strategies
- Activity monitoring
- Recognizing overuse symptoms

**5. Gait & Mobility Optimization**
- Gait assessment for changes
- Orthotic review:
  - May need new/updated braces
  - Support for newly weakened muscles
  - Assistive devices evaluation
  - Canes, walkers, or wheelchair if needed
- Fall prevention
- Safety adaptations

**6. Functional Adaptation**
- Identify activities becoming difficult
- Teach compensatory strategies
- Assistive device training
- Environmental modifications
- ADL adaptations
- Acceptance of limitations

**7. Psychosocial Support**
- Address depression/anxiety
- Cope with progressive decline
- Adjustment to changing abilities
- Support groups (PPS-specific very helpful)
- Counseling
- Advocacy support
- Quality of life optimization

**8. Home Exercise Program**
- Simple, sustainable exercises
- Low-intensity, low-repetition
- Emphasis on consistency, not intensity
- 2-4 exercises
- Performed 3 days/week with rest days
- Clear instructions (written, pictures)
- Regular reassessment and modification

## 7. KEY POINTS - POLIOMYELITIS & PPS

✓ **Poliovirus** - now rare due to vaccination
✓ **Asymmetric paralysis** - hallmark of acute polio
✓ **Motor neuron loss** - causes flaccid paralysis
✓ **PPS onset** - 15-40+ years after acute polio
✓ **Fatigue disproportionate** - key feature of PPS
✓ **Avoid overuse** - can accelerate decline in PPS
✓ **Activity pacing critical** - energy conservation essential
✓ **Weak strengthening** - not high-intensity
✓ **Aquatic exercise excellent** - for PPS
✓ **Progressive decline expected** - but rate varies
✓ **Adapt and modify** - activities and equipment
