# Unit III: Motor Neuron Disease - Comprehensive Study Guide

## 1. DEFINITION & PATHOPHYSIOLOGY

### Definition
Motor Neuron Disease (MND) is a group of progressive neurological disorders characterized by degeneration of motor neurons, resulting in progressive weakness, muscle atrophy, and eventual paralysis. The most common form is Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis (ALS).

### Types of MND

**Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis (ALS) - Most Common (90%)**
- Progressive degeneration of upper and lower motor neurons
- Rapidly progressive
- Fatal disease (2-5 year median survival from diagnosis)
- Also called Lou Gehrig's disease

**Progressive Muscular Atrophy (PMA)**
- Lower motor neuron involvement primarily
- Generally slower progression than ALS
- Better prognosis than classic ALS

**Primary Lateral Sclerosis (PLS)**
- Upper motor neuron involvement primarily
- Slower progression
- Better prognosis

**Progressive Bulbar Palsy (PBP)**
- Initial involvement of bulbar muscles (speech, swallowing)
- Progressive to generalized weakness
- Variant of ALS

### Pathophysiology (Using ALS as Example)

**Motor Neuron Degeneration**
- Loss of upper motor neurons (motor cortex, corticospinal tract)
- Loss of lower motor neurons (spinal cord, brainstem)
- Neuronal cell death (apoptosis)
- Protein aggregation (SOD1 protein, TDP-43)

**Cellular Mechanisms** (Complex, incompletely understood)
- Excitotoxicity (glutamate excess)
- Oxidative stress
- Mitochondrial dysfunction
- Protein misfolding
- Inflammation (microglial activation)
- Loss of neurotrophic factors
- RNA processing abnormalities

**Result**
- Progressive weakness
- Muscle atrophy
- Spasticity (from UMN loss)
- Fasciculations (from LMN degeneration)
- Eventual paralysis
- Respiratory failure

## 2. ETIOLOGY & RISK FACTORS

### Genetic Forms (5-10% familial)
- SOD1 gene mutation (most common genetic form)
- C9orf72 repeat expansion
- FUS gene mutations
- TDP-43 mutations
- Other genetic mutations

### Sporadic Forms (90%)
- Cause unknown (likely multifactorial)
- Environmental factors possibly involved
- Age-related neuronal vulnerability

### Risk Factors for Sporadic ALS
- Age (peak onset 55-65 years)
- Male gender (1.5:1 male predominance)
- Family history (even in sporadic form)
- Environmental exposures (pesticides, heavy metals) - unproven
- Smoking (may increase risk)
- Physical trauma (controversial)

## 3. CLINICAL FEATURES & PRESENTATION

### Onset & Pattern

**Age at Onset**
- Typically 50-60 years (can occur younger or older)
- Juvenile-onset ALS: <25 years (rare)
- Rapid progression: Death within 2-3 years
- Slower progression: Up to 10+ years (rare)

**Initial Symptoms** (Variable)

**Limb-Onset (70% of cases)**
- Asymmetric weakness (one limb initially)
- Difficulty with fine motor tasks
- Difficulty with walking
- Clumsiness
- Foot drop
- Hand weakness/dropping objects

**Bulbar-Onset (25% of cases)**
- Speech difficulty (dysarthria, slurred speech)
- Difficulty swallowing (dysphagia)
- Nasal voice quality
- Facial weakness
- Jaw weakness
- Generally faster progression

**Respiratory-Onset (Rare, 5%)**
- Dyspnea on exertion
- Shortness of breath
- Sleep-disordered breathing
- Orthopnea (shortness of breath when lying down)

### Progressive Motor Features

**Upper Motor Neuron Signs**
- Increased muscle tone (spasticity)
- Hyperreflexia (brisk reflexes)
- Positive Babinski sign
- Weakness (stiffness)
- Clonus

**Lower Motor Neuron Signs**
- Muscle atrophy (wasting)
- Fasciculations (muscle twitching - visible under skin)
- Weakness (flaccidity)
- Areflexia (absent reflexes)
- Cramping

**Pattern of Progression**
- Progressive weakness (limb or bulbar)
- Spreads to other regions over time
- Ultimately generalized weakness
- Respiratory muscles affected eventually (major cause of death)

### Non-Motor Features

**Cognitive Changes**
- ALS-related frontotemporal dementia (10-15% of ALS)
- Cognitive decline (executive function)
- Behavioral changes
- Can occur without motor symptoms (FTD-ALS spectrum)

**Emotional Features**
- Pseudobulbar affect (emotional lability - inappropriate crying/laughing)
- Depression (common)
- Anxiety

**Sensory**
- Sensory loss unusual in ALS (if present, suggests different diagnosis)
- Pain (can occur from muscle cramping, immobility)

**Autonomic**
- Generally preserved until late disease
- Ocular movements usually preserved (distinguishes from other conditions)

## 4. DIAGNOSIS & ASSESSMENT

### Diagnostic Criteria (El Escorial Criteria - Modified)

**Diagnosis of ALS requires:**
1. Evidence of lower motor neuron (LMN) degeneration (EMG/clinical)
2. Evidence of upper motor neuron (UMN) degeneration (clinical)
3. Progressive spread of symptoms (clinical/electrophysiological)
4. No other explanation for symptoms

### Diagnostic Tests

**Electromyography (EMG)**
- Gold standard for diagnosis
- Shows denervation (LMN signs):
  - Fibrillations
  - Positive sharp waves
  - Reduced motor unit recruitment
  - Large amplitude motor units (reinnervation)
- Myopathic pattern absent (distinguishes from muscle disease)

**MRI Spine/Brain**
- Rule out other causes (tumors, structural lesions)
- May show corticospinal tract changes (hyperintensity on T2)
- Not diagnostic but supportive

**Blood Tests**
- Creatine kinase (normal or mildly elevated in ALS)
- Tests for alternative diagnoses
- Genetic testing (if familial)

### Clinical Assessment

**ALS Functional Rating Scale (ALSFRS-R)**
- 12 items assessing function
- Speech, salivation, swallowing, handwriting, cutting food
- Dressing/hygiene, turning in bed, walking, climbing stairs, dyspnea, orthopnea, respiratory insufficiency
- 0-4 scale per item
- Total 0-48 points
- Useful for monitoring progression
- Guides management decisions

**Manual Muscle Testing (MMT)**
- Standard 0-5 scale
- Document all major muscle groups
- Identify pattern
- Baseline and follow-up comparison

**Functional Assessment**
- ADL independence
- Mobility status
- Respiratory function:
  - Vital capacity (FVC)
  - Dyspnea assessment
  - Sleep apnea screening
- Speech/swallowing function
- Cognitive assessment (MMSE, MoCA)

**Gait Assessment** (Early/mid-disease)
- Walking ability
- Need for assistive devices
- Fall risk

## 5. MANAGEMENT PROTOCOLS

### A. Medical Management (Overview)

**Disease-Modifying Therapy**
- Riluzole: FDA-approved, extends survival ~2-3 months
- Edaravone: FDA-approved, may slow progression ~25%
- Emerging: Gene therapy, stem cell therapy (experimental)

**Symptomatic Management**
- Spasticity: baclofen, tizanidine, botulinum toxin
- Cramps: quinine, magnesium
- Pseudobulbar affect: medications available
- Depression/anxiety: antidepressants
- Sleep issues: medications
- Breathing support: BiPAP, mechanical ventilation

**Nutritional Support**
- High-calorie diet (ALS increases metabolic rate)
- G-tube placement if swallowing compromised
- Fluid management

### B. Physical Therapy Management

**Goals**
1. Maintain function and independence as long as possible
2. Manage symptoms (spasticity, pain, cramping)
3. Prevent secondary complications (contractures, pressure ulcers)
4. Optimize mobility and safety
5. Maintain quality of life
6. Psychosocial support

**Principles**
- Symptom-focused (disease-modifying limits available)
- Functional, meaningful activities
- Activity pacing (avoid overuse fatigue)
- Equipment modification as disease progresses
- Regular reassessment and adaptation
- Anticipatory guidance (what to expect)
- Interdisciplinary approach

**Interventions**

**1. Stretching & Contracture Prevention**
- Daily gentle stretching
- Hold 30-60 seconds
- Key areas: ankle plantarflexors, hip flexors, hamstrings, shoulder muscles
- Nighttime splinting (orthoses)
- Especially important as mobility declines

**2. Gentle Strengthening** (Early to mid-disease)
- Low-intensity exercises
- Avoid fatigue
- Short sessions
- Frequency: 3-4 days/week
- Modalities:
  - Active ROM
  - Light resistance
  - Aquatic exercise (excellent - buoyancy support)
  - Tai chi (if appropriate)
- Monitor: Overuse can cause cramping

**3. Spasticity Management**
- Stretching (as above)
- Heat application
- Tone-reducing techniques
- Positioning (support and comfort)
- Coordination with medical management

**4. Pain & Cramping Management**
- Stretching (prevents cramping)
- Gentle massage
- Heat application
- Proper positioning
- Medications (quinine, magnesium)
- Activity modification

**5. Mobility Maintenance**

**Early Disease (Ambulatory)**
- Gait training
- Balance training
- Assistive device training (cane, walker)
- Orthotic prescription (AFO for foot drop)
- Stairs, inclines, uneven surfaces
- Fall prevention
- Energy conservation

**Mid Disease (Mobility Declining)**
- Transition planning (to wheelchair)
- Transfer training (bed, chair, toilet)
- Adaptive equipment introduction
- Home modification planning
- Environmental assessment

**Late Disease (Wheelchair/Bed-Bound)**
- Wheelchair positioning
- Pressure ulcer prevention
- Transfer assistance training
- Bed positioning
- Comfort optimization

**6. Respiratory Support** (Coordination with pulmonary team)
- Breathing exercises (early-mid disease)
- Cough assistance techniques
- Equipment: BiPAP, mechanical ventilation
- Positioning for optimal breathing
- Sleep positioning
- Monitoring for respiratory compromise

**7. Speech/Swallowing Coordination** (With SLP)
- Referral to speech pathology
- Communication aids (if speech affected)
- Swallowing assessment
- Dysphagia management
- G-tube coordination (if placed)
- Aspiration prevention

**8. Activities of Daily Living Adaptation**
- Assistive devices (reachers, adaptive utensils)
- Environmental modification
- Compensatory strategies
- Energy conservation
- Caregiver training
- Regular reassessment as abilities change

**9. Equipment Prescription & Adaptation**
- Orthoses (AFO)
- Canes, walkers, wheelchairs
- Pressure-relief cushions and mattresses
- Grab bars, handrails
- Hospital bed
- Lift equipment
- Positioning devices
- Progressive modification as disease advances

**10. Psychosocial Support**
- Acknowledge devastation of diagnosis
- Depression screening and support
- Coping strategies
- Support groups (ALS-specific very valuable)
- Counseling/psychology referral
- Spiritual support if desired
- End-of-life planning discussion
- Goals of care conversation

**11. Caregiver Support & Education**
- Transfer techniques and safety
- Proper body mechanics
- ADL assistance strategies
- Equipment use and care
- Recognizing complications
- Emotional support
- Respite care options
- Support groups for caregivers

**12. Home Exercise Program**
- Simple stretching exercises
- Gentle ROM
- 2-4 exercises
- Performed daily or 5-6 days/week
- Written instructions with pictures
- Regular modification as abilities change
- Caregiver involvement when needed

## 6. PT EVALUATION POINTS FOR PRACTICALS

### Assessment Components
- Determine pattern (limb vs bulbar onset, UMN vs LMN signs)
- Comprehensive strength testing (MMT all muscles)
- ALSFRS-R score (functional baseline)
- Tone assessment (spasticity)
- ROM/contracture assessment
- Gait analysis (if ambulatory)
- Mobility assessment
- Respiratory screening
- Identify current limitations

### Treatment Planning
- Phase-appropriate goals
- Symptom-focused interventions
- Equipment needs assessment
- Caregiver education
- Anticipatory guidance
- Supportive, realistic approach
- Multidisciplinary coordination

---

## 7. KEY POINTS TO REMEMBER

✓ **Progressive, fatal disease** - no cure currently
✓ **Motor neuron loss** - both UMN and LMN (typically)
✓ **Variable presentation** - limb vs bulbar onset
✓ **Rapidly progressive** - median survival 2-5 years
✓ **Weakness primary symptom** - progresses systemically
✓ **Respiratory failure** - eventual cause of death
✓ **Cognition variable** - preserved in most
✓ **Ocular movements preserved** - helps distinguish from other diseases
✓ **PT symptom-focused** - not disease-modifying
✓ **Early equipment** - anticipate needs
✓ **Stretching critical** - prevent contractures
✓ **Caregiver support essential** - heavy burden
✓ **Psychosocial support vital** - devastating diagnosis
✓ **Anticipatory guidance** - prepare for progression
✓ **Goals of care** - important to discuss early
