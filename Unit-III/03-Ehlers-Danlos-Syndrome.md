# Unit III: Ehlers-Danlos Syndrome - Comprehensive Study Guide

## 1. DEFINITION & PATHOPHYSIOLOGY

### Definition
Ehlers-Danlos Syndrome (EDS) is a group of inherited connective tissue disorders characterized by defects in collagen structure or synthesis. This results in abnormal skin, joint, and connective tissue properties, leading to hypermobility, skin fragility, and tissue scarring.

### Types (13 Recognized Types)

**Classical EDS (cEDS)**
- Most common form
- Collagen V defects
- Autosomal dominant
- Skin hyperextensibility, velvety skin, poor wound healing
- Joint hypermobility

**Hypermobile EDS (hEDS)**
- Most common type currently recognized
- Generalized joint hypermobility
- Gastrointestinal and autonomic symptoms
- Minimal skin findings

**Vascular EDS (vEDS)**
- Most serious; high mortality
- Arterial rupture risk
- Hollow organ rupture
- Collagen III defects

**Kyphoscoliotic EDS (kEDS)**
- Lysl hydroxylase deficiency
- Severe progressive kyphoscoliosis
- Skin hyperextensibility
- Joint hypermobility

**Other Types**
- Arthrochalasia EDS
- Dermatosparaxis EDS
- And 8 additional rare types

### Pathophysiology

**Collagen Defects**
- Abnormal collagen cross-linking
- Disrupted collagen fibril organization
- Reduced collagen synthesis or secretion
- Defective collagen processing enzymes

**Result**
- Weakened connective tissue
- Increased tissue extensibility
- Joint hypermobility and instability
- Skin fragility
- Poor wound healing
- Vascular complications (in vascular type)

## 2. CLINICAL FEATURES & PRESENTATION

### General Features

**Skin Manifestations**
- Hyperextensibility: skin stretches easily and returns slowly
- Velvety texture (classical type)
- Fragility: prone to scarring and poor wound healing
- Easy bruising
- Atrophic scars (thin, shiny)
- Poor wound healing

**Joint Manifestations**
- Generalized joint hypermobility
- Recurrent joint subluxations/dislocations
- Chronic pain and stiffness
- Early-onset osteoarthritis
- "Banana-shaped" fingers
- Ability to perform unusual contortions

**Skeletal Manifestations**
- Kyphoscoliosis (especially in kEDS)
- Chest deformity
- Foot deformities (pes planus, pes cavus)
- Delayed motor milestones in children

**Cardiovascular Manifestations** (Variable by type)
- Mitral valve prolapse (common)
- Arterial rupture (vascular EDS)
- Aortic root dilation
- Aneurysms

**Gastrointestinal Manifestations** (hEDS)
- Reflux
- Dysphagia
- Gastric dysmotility
- Constipation or diarrhea
- Abdominal pain
- Hernias

**Autonomic Dysfunction** (hEDS)
- Orthostatic intolerance
- Palpitations
- Presyncope/syncope
- Dysautonomia features

**Neurological Features**
- Proprioceptive deficits
- Balance problems
- Anxiety
- Chronic pain (widespread)

## 3. ASSESSMENT PROCEDURES & CLINICAL TESTS

### Clinical Examination

**Beighton Hypermobility Score**
- 9-point scale assessing joint flexibility
- Score >5 suggests hypermobility
- 1 point for each of:
  - Thumb to forearm (bilateral)
  - Index finger hyperextension (bilateral)
  - Elbow hyperextension (bilateral)
  - Knee hyperextension (bilateral)
  - Forward bend with knees straight (1 point if achieves full forward bend)

**Brighton Criteria**
- More comprehensive assessment
- Includes Beighton score plus
- Skin elasticity test
- Skin striae assessment
- Bruising
- Oral manifestations

**Range of Motion Assessment**
- Goniometric measurement of hypermobile joints
- Compare to normal ranges
- Document hypermobility

**Skin Examination**
- Hyperextensibility: pull skin and assess return
- Texture: velvet-like vs normal
- Scarring pattern: assess healing quality
- Bruising: easy or spontaneous

**Proprioceptive Assessment**
- Joint position sense testing
- May be impaired in EDS
- Relevant to fall risk and coordination

**Balance & Coordination**
- Due to proprioceptive deficits
- Tandem stance
- Single-leg stance
- Romberg test
- Dynamic balance activities

### Diagnostic Testing

**Genetic Testing**
- Confirms diagnosis
- Identifies specific type
- Gene sequencing for specific types
- Valuable for family counseling

**Skin Biopsy**
- Collagen structure assessment
- Electron microscopy
- Particularly useful for vascular EDS

**Cardiovascular Screening**
- Echocardiography (assess cardiac valves, aortic root)
- Blood pressure monitoring
- ECG if indicated

### Functional Assessment

**Pain Assessment**
- Visual analog scale or numeric rating scale
- Pain location and pattern
- Chronic widespread pain common

**Fatigue Severity**
- Fatigue is common
- Impact on function
- Correlation with activity level

**Activities of Daily Living Assessment**
- Functional limitations
- Joint instability effects
- Impact on independence

## 4. MANAGEMENT PROTOCOLS

### A. Physical Therapy Management

**Goals**
1. Joint stability and protection
2. Pain management
3. Proprioceptive training
4. Functional optimization
5. Prevent secondary complications
6. Education and activity modification

**General Principles**
- Avoid aggressive stretching (tissue fragile)
- Focus on stability rather than flexibility
- Progressive resistance training
- Low-impact activities
- Activity modification
- Joint protection strategies

**Interventions**

**1. Proprioceptive & Balance Training**
- Proprioceptive neuromuscular facilitation (PNF)
- Balance activities:
  - Standing unsupported
  - Tandem stance
  - Perturbation training
  - Dynamic balance activities
- Sensory retraining
- Weight shifts
- Controlled unstable surface activities (carefully)
- Closed-chain exercises

**2. Strengthening Program**
- Isometric exercises (initial phase)
- Progressive resistance (graduated)
- Focus on joint stabilizers:
  - Rotator cuff (shoulder stability)
  - Core stability (abdominals, back)
  - Hip stabilizers
  - Ankle/foot stabilizers
- Functional movement patterns
- Avoid eccentric stress (tissue fragility risk)

**3. Joint Stabilization Training**
- Muscle guarding techniques
- Neuromuscular re-education
- Conscious stability during movement
- Proprioceptive feedback
- Functional activities with attention to form

**4. Orthotic Support**
- Bracing for hypermobile joints:
  - Ankle braces/orthoses
  - Knee braces (especially for patellofemoral involvement)
  - Shoulder taping or bracing
  - Wrist supports
  - Custom orthotics (may improve proprioception)
- Compression garments (may help with proprioception)

**5. Pain Management**
- Manual therapy (gentle, avoid aggressive mobilization)
- Soft tissue mobilization (respecting tissue fragility)
- Modalities: heat, TENS, transcutaneous electrical stimulation
- Postural correction
- Activity pacing
- Relaxation techniques

**6. Postural Training**
- Maintain good postural alignment
- Prevent compensatory patterns
- Core strengthening
- Awareness training (mirror feedback)
- Ergonomic education

**7. Gait Training**
- Address gait abnormalities (if present)
- Proprioceptive cueing
- Mechanical efficiency
- Foot alignment
- Stride normalization

**8. Energy Conservation & Activity Modification**
- Teach pacing strategies
- Activity grading
- Identify movement patterns causing pain/instability
- Modify daily activities
- Use of assistive devices when beneficial
- Fatigue management

**9. Cardiovascular Fitness**
- Low-impact activities:
  - Walking
  - Swimming (excellent for EDS - support without stress)
  - Cycling (stationary)
  - Aquatic exercise
- Gradual progression
- Avoid high-impact activities
- Monitor for autonomic symptoms

**10. Home Exercise Program**
- Focused stabilization exercises
- Daily activities
- 4-6 exercises maximum
- Progressive difficulty
- Instruction on proper form (critical for safety)
- Photographs/video instruction
- Regular reassessment

**11. Psychosocial Support**
- Chronic pain management
- Cope with functional limitations
- Support groups
- Counseling for anxiety, depression
- Acceptance of condition

### B. Precautions in Physical Therapy

**Avoid:**
- Aggressive stretching (tissue damage risk)
- High-impact activities (joint stress)
- Forceful manipulations
- Valsalva maneuvers (vascular EDS risk)
- Excessive fatigue
- Extreme ranges of motion
- Hypermobility exercises

**Monitor:**
- Vital signs (particularly vascular EDS)
- Joint stress
- Pain levels
- Tissue response to exercise
- Signs of joint instability or subluxation
- Cardiovascular symptoms

## 5. PT EVALUATION POINTS FOR PRACTICALS

### Assessment Focus
- Beighton score
- Pattern of hypermobility
- Pain location and severity
- Proprioceptive deficits
- Balance and coordination issues
- Joint stability/instability
- Skin fragility concerns
- Cardiovascular screening (awareness)

### Treatment Planning
- Emphasize stability over mobility
- Joint protection strategies
- Proprioceptive training
- Gentle strengthening
- Activity modification
- Pain management
- Safety precautions

---

## 6. KEY POINTS TO REMEMBER

✓ **Focus on stability, NOT flexibility** - joints already hypermobile
✓ **Tissue fragility** - avoid aggressive techniques
✓ **Proprioceptive deficit** - balance and coordination training essential
✓ **Joint protection** - bracing, taping, orthoses helpful
✓ **Avoid high-impact** - joint damage risk
✓ **Strengthening crucial** - stabilizes hypermobile joints
✓ **Pain management important** - chronic widespread pain
✓ **Type matters** - vascular EDS most serious, needs monitoring
✓ **Activity modification key** - functional independence
✓ **Psychological support** - cope with chronic condition
✓ **Multidisciplinary approach** - coordinate with medical team
✓ **Aquatic exercise beneficial** - support without stress
