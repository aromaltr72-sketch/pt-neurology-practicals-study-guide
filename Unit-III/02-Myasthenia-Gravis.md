# Unit III: Myasthenia Gravis - Comprehensive Study Guide

## 1. DEFINITION & PATHOPHYSIOLOGY

### Definition
Myasthenia Gravis (MG) is an autoimmune neuromuscular disorder characterized by weakness and fatigability of skeletal muscles. It results from antibodies attacking the neuromuscular junction, specifically targeting acetylcholine receptors (AChR) or muscle-specific kinase (MuSK).

### Pathophysiology

**Autoimmune Mechanism**
- Production of IgG antibodies against nicotinic acetylcholine receptors (85% of patients)
- Antibodies bind to AChR at neuromuscular junction
- Complement-mediated destruction of motor end plate
- Blockade of acetylcholine access to receptors
- Increased turnover and cross-linking of receptors
- Result: Decreased number of functional AChRs (up to 80% reduction)

**MuSK-Antibody Positive MG (15% of patients)**
- Antibodies against muscle-specific kinase
- Different pathophysiology but similar clinical presentation
- Often seronegative for AChR antibodies

**Clinical Consequence**
- Impaired neuromuscular transmission
- Failure of muscle to sustain contraction
- Fatigue and weakness with use
- Recovery with rest

## 2. ETIOLOGY & CLASSIFICATION

### Classification

**By Age of Onset**
- **Early-onset MG:** <40 years, female predominance (2:1)
- **Late-onset MG:** >50 years, male predominance
- **Juvenile MG:** <15 years (rare)

**By Thymic Involvement**
- **Thymoma-associated MG:** 10-15% have thymoma
- **Thymic hyperplasia:** 50-60% have thymic abnormalities
- **Seronegative MG:** No antibodies detected (10-15%)

**By Severity (Osserman Classification)**
- **Type I:** Ocular myasthenia only
- **Type II:** Mild systemic myasthenia (IIA) or moderate systemic (IIB)
- **Type III:** Acute fulminant myasthenia
- **Type IV:** Late severe myasthenia

## 3. CLINICAL FEATURES & PRESENTATION

### Cardinal Features
- **Weakness and fatigability** of skeletal muscles
- Symptoms worsen with activity, improve with rest
- Fluctuating course throughout day
- Typically worse in evening

### Pattern of Muscle Involvement

**Early Manifestations (Ocular - 50% of patients)**
- Ptosis (drooping eyelids)
- Diplopia (double vision)
- Ocular weakness only in 15% at diagnosis
- Progresses to systemic disease in 80% within 2 years

**Bulbar Symptoms**
- Facial weakness
- Dysarthria (speech difficulty)
- Dysphagia (swallowing difficulty) - risk of aspiration
- Weak smile or grimace
- Nasal quality to speech

**Skeletal Muscle Involvement**
- Neck flexors: difficulty holding head up
- Shoulder/arm weakness: difficulty reaching overhead
- Hip/leg weakness: difficulty climbing stairs, walking
- Typically proximal > distal pattern
- Asymmetric initially, becomes symmetric

**Respiratory Muscles**
- Dyspnea (shortness of breath)
- Weakness of diaphragm and intercostal muscles
- Myasthenic crisis: severe respiratory weakness requiring ventilation
- Occurs in 10-20% of patients at some point

### Clinical Characteristics

**Pattern of Weakness**
- Fluctuating: varies throughout day and days
- Fatigable: worse with repetitive use
- Improves with rest and anticholinergic medications
- No sensory loss
- Reflexes normal

**Triggers for Worsening**
- Infection
- Stress and emotional upset
- Heat and fever
- Menstrual cycle changes
- Certain medications (aminoglycosides, quinidine, procainamide)
- Surgery and anesthesia

**Associated Autoimmune Conditions**
- Thyroid disease (5-10%)
- Systemic lupus erythematosus
- Rheumatoid arthritis
- Polymyositis
- Sjögren's syndrome

## 4. ASSESSMENT PROCEDURES & CLINICAL TESTS

### Clinical Assessment

**Myasthenic Manifestations Test**
- Sustained upgaze (30 seconds): ptosis worsens
- Sustained hand grip: weakness increases
- Repeated stepping: progressive weakness
- Repeated smiling: facial weakness becomes obvious
- Count aloud to 50: voice fatigues

**Ice Pack Test**
- Place ice pack over eyelids for 2-3 minutes
- Positive test: Brief improvement in ptosis
- Sensitivity: 90% for ocular MG
- Simple bedside test

**Edrophonium (Tensilon) Test**
- Administration of acetylcholinesterase inhibitor IV
- Produces brief improvement of symptoms
- Less commonly used now (availability issues)
- Sensitivity: 60-95%

### Antibody Testing

**Serum AChR Antibodies**
- Positive in 85% of generalized MG
- Positive in 50% of purely ocular MG
- Highly specific (99%) for MG
- Correlates with disease severity

**Anti-MuSK Antibodies**
- Positive in 40% of seronegative patients
- Different clinical features (more bulbar and respiratory)
- Often more refractory to treatment

### Electrophysiological Testing

**Repetitive Nerve Stimulation (RNS)**
- Shows decremental response (progressively smaller muscle action potentials)
- Sensitivity: 50-70% in generalized MG, 10% in ocular MG
- Useful for diagnosis and monitoring

**Single Fiber Electromyography (SFEMG)**
- Gold standard for diagnosis
- Shows increased "jitter" and blocking
- Sensitivity: >90% in generalized MG, >90% in ocular MG
- Specific for neuromuscular junction dysfunction

### Imaging Studies

**Chest CT or MRI**
- To screen for thymoma
- Important for treatment planning
- 10-15% of MG patients have thymoma

### Functional Assessment

**Quantitative Myasthenia Gravis Score (QMG)**
- 13 items assessing various muscle groups
- Scoring: 0-39 points (higher = more severe)
- Objective measure of weakness
- Good reliability and validity
- Useful for monitoring progression and treatment response

**Myasthenia Gravis Composite Scale (MGC)**
- Combines objective and patient-reported measures
- 10 items
- More efficient than QMG
- Includes functional activities

**Manual Muscle Testing (MMT)**
- Standard 0-5 scale
- Document pattern (ocular, bulbar, limb distribution)
- Assess fatigability by repeated testing
- Baseline and follow-up

## 5. MANAGEMENT PROTOCOLS

### A. Medical Management (Overview)

**Anticholinesterase Medications**
- Pyridostigmine: inhibits breakdown of acetylcholine
- First-line symptomatic treatment
- Provides modest benefit
- Side effects: cholinergic toxicity (GI upset, increased secretions)

**Immunosuppressive Therapy**
- Corticosteroids (prednisone)
- Azathioprine
- Mycophenolate mofetil
- Tacrolimus
- For moderate to severe disease

**Acute Treatment**
- Intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG)
- Plasmapheresis
- For myasthenic crisis or acute exacerbations

**Thymectomy**
- Surgical removal of thymus gland
- Recommended for most patients
- Particularly beneficial if thymoma present

### B. Physical Therapy Management

**Goals**
1. Maximize functional independence
2. Optimize energy conservation
3. Maintain strength and range of motion
4. Prevent secondary complications
5. Educate on activity pacing

**Assessment Considerations**
- Fluctuating nature of disease
- Impact of activity level
- Timing of medications
- Fatigue patterns

**General Principles**
- Exercise during times of peak medication effect
- Avoid excessive fatigue
- Pacing and energy conservation
- Frequent rest periods
- Gradual progression

**Interventions**

**1. Range of Motion & Stretching**
- Gentle passive and active ROM
- Prevent contractures
- Not fatiguing
- Frequency: Daily if tolerated

**2. Low-Intensity Strengthening**
- Isometric exercises (hold without movement)
- Avoid high-repetition resistance
- Light weights or body weight only
- Short sessions
- Rest between exercises

**3. Functional Training**
- ADL tasks practiced during strong times
- Teach energy conservation techniques:
  - Sit while performing tasks
  - Combine activities requiring similar muscle groups
  - Rest periods between activities
  - Prioritize important activities

**4. Gait Training**
- If leg weakness present
- Assistive devices: canes, walkers
- Proper body mechanics
- Energy conservation

**5. Breathing & Postural Support**
- Breathing exercises (diaphragmatic breathing)
- Posture support to assist with trunk muscles
- Spinal support if needed
- Monitor for respiratory compromise

**6. Fatigue Management**
- Education on activity pacing
- Identifying triggers
- Scheduling rest periods
- Balancing activity and rest
- Energy monitoring techniques

**7. Education & Psychosocial Support**
- Unpredictability of disease
- Importance of medication compliance
- Recognizing myasthenic crisis warning signs:
  - Severe weakness
  - Respiratory difficulty
  - Difficulty swallowing
  - Slurred speech
  - Ptosis and diplopia severe
- Avoiding triggers (stress, heat, infections)
- Support groups
- Psychological counseling if needed

**8. Home Exercise Program**
- Simple, well-tolerated exercises
- Performed during peak medication times
- Written with pictures
- 3-4 exercises maximum
- Emphasis on consistency rather than intensity

**Home Safety**
- Prevent falls (weakness, fatigue)
- Lighting and accessibility
- Assistive devices
- Environmental modifications

## 6. PT EVALUATION POINTS FOR PRACTICALS

### Case Presentation
- Type and severity of MG
- Pattern of muscle involvement (ocular, bulbar, systemic)
- Osserman classification
- Medication regimen
- Time since diagnosis
- Complications present

### Assessment
- Perform ice pack test if ocular involvement
- Assess for fatigability (repeat manual testing)
- Use QMG or MGC scale
- Document pattern of weakness
- Screen for respiratory compromise
- Assess functional abilities
- Swallowing screen (dysphagia risk)

### Treatment Planning
- Set realistic, achievable goals
- Emphasize energy conservation
- Design low-intensity exercise program
- Coordinate timing with medication effect
- Include family/caregiver education
- Safety precautions for myasthenic crisis

---

## 7. KEY POINTS TO REMEMBER

✓ **Fatigue is hallmark** - symptoms worsen with use, improve with rest
✓ **Fluctuating course** - worse in evening, varies day to day
✓ **Exercise timing critical** - during peak medication effect
✓ **Avoid overuse** - risk of myasthenic crisis
✓ **Respiratory monitoring essential** - watch for compromise
✓ **Swallowing assessment important** - aspiration risk
✓ **Energy conservation key** - teach pacing strategies
✓ **Autoimmune nature** - educate on triggers
✓ **Variable presentation** - ocular, bulbar, or generalized
✓ **Medication-dependent** - timing affects PT sessions
✓ **Crisis awareness** - recognize warning signs
✓ **Prognosis generally good** - with proper management
