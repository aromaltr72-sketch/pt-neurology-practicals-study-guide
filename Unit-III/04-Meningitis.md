# Unit III: Meningitis - Comprehensive Study Guide

## 1. DEFINITION & PATHOPHYSIOLOGY

### Definition
Meningitis is inflammation of the meninges (the membranes surrounding the brain and spinal cord). It can be caused by viral, bacterial, or fungal organisms, with bacterial meningitis being the most serious form requiring urgent treatment.

### Types of Meningitis

**Bacterial Meningitis (Most serious)**
- Rapid onset
- High mortality (10-15%) and morbidity
- Requires immediate hospitalization and antibiotics
- Survivors often have permanent neurological sequelae
- Common organisms: Neisseria meningitidis, Streptococcus pneumoniae, Haemophilus influenzae

**Viral Meningitis (Aseptic Meningitis)**
- Usually self-limited
- Better prognosis
- Most common form
- Enteroviruses most common cause
- Most recover completely

**Fungal Meningitis**
- Subacute onset
- Often in immunocompromised patients
- Cryptococcus most common
- Requires prolonged treatment

## 2. CLINICAL FEATURES & PRESENTATION

### Classic Triad
1. **Fever** (usually high grade)
2. **Headache** (severe, persistent)
3. **Neck Stiffness** (meningeal sign)

### Additional Symptoms

**General Symptoms**
- Malaise and body aches
- Muscle pain
- Chills
- Nausea and vomiting
- Sensitivity to light (photophobia)
- Sensitivity to sound (phonophobia)
- Altered consciousness (confusion, lethargy)

**Neurological Signs**
- Altered mental status/confusion
- Lethargy or stupor
- Seizures (20-40% of cases)
- Cranial nerve palsies (CN VI most common)
- Focal neurological deficits
- Loss of consciousness

**Bacterial Meningitis Specific**
- Petechial or purpuric rash (Meningococcemia)
- More rapid progression than viral
- Higher fever
- More severe symptoms
- Can lead to septic shock

## 3. ASSESSMENT & DIAGNOSIS

### Clinical Examination

**Meningeal Signs**

**Nuchal Rigidity (Neck Stiffness)**
- Resistance to passive neck flexion
- Patient cannot touch chin to chest
- Classic meningeal sign
- Sensitivity: 70-80% for bacterial meningitis

**Kernig's Sign**
- Patient supine, hip and knee flexed 90 degrees
- Pain or resistance when knee extended
- Positive in meningitis
- Specificity: >95%

**Brudzinski's Sign**
- Positive if passive neck flexion causes hip/knee flexion
- Indicates meningeal irritation
- Sensitivity: 50-60%

**Petechial Rash Assessment** (if present)
- Purple-red non-blanching rash
- Suggests meningococcal meningitis
- Medical emergency indicator
- Associated with sepsis

### Diagnostic Tests

**Lumbar Puncture (CSF Analysis) - Gold Standard**
- Cerebrospinal fluid examination

**CSF Findings in Bacterial Meningitis**
- High protein (>250 mg/dL)
- Low glucose (CSF:plasma ratio <40%)
- Pleocytosis (>1000 cells/μL, predominantly PMNs)
- Positive Gram stain or culture
- Turbid/cloudy appearance

**CSF Findings in Viral Meningitis**
- Slightly elevated protein (50-100 mg/dL)
- Normal or slightly low glucose
- Pleocytosis (100-1000 cells/μL, predominantly lymphocytes)
- Negative cultures
- Clear or slightly turbid

**Blood Cultures**
- Positive in bacterial meningitis
- Should be done before antibiotics

**Imaging Studies**
- CT or MRI brain
- Assess for complications:
  - Subdural empyema
  - Ventriculitis
  - Hydrocephalus
  - Cerebral edema

## 4. COMPLICATIONS

### Acute Phase Complications
- Seizures
- Cerebral edema
- Hydrocephalus
- Subdural empyema
- Septic shock
- Multi-organ failure
- Disseminated intravascular coagulation (DIC)

### Chronic/Sequelae
- Sensorineural hearing loss (10-30%)
- Cognitive impairment (20-30%)
- Motor deficits/hemiparesis
- Seizure disorder
- Hydrocephalus (communicating)
- Subdural effusion
- Cranial nerve palsies
- Vision impairment
- Learning disabilities (children)

---

## 5. MANAGEMENT PROTOCOLS

### A. Medical Management (Brief Overview)

**Antibiotics**
- Empiric: Ceftriaxone, vancomycin ± ampicillin
- Started immediately (even before culture confirmation)
- Duration: Typically 7-10 days

**Supportive Care**
- Fluid management
- Fever control
- Seizure management
- Ventilatory support if needed
- Osmotic therapy (mannitol, hypertonic saline) for cerebral edema

**Dexamethasone**
- Often given with antibiotics
- Reduces neurological complications and mortality

### B. Physical Therapy Management

**PT Role Depends on Phase**

**Acute Phase**
- Minimal PT typically (patient critically ill)
- Passive ROM if appropriate
- Positioning to prevent complications
- Monitor vitals closely
- Coordination with medical team

**Early Recovery Phase**
- Assess for neurological deficits
- Identify any motor impairment
- Begin gentle ROM
- Early mobilization when appropriate
- Assessment for complications

**Rehabilitation Phase** (If neurological sequelae)

**If Motor Deficits (Hemiparesis, Weakness)**
- Strength assessment (MMT)
- Motor retraining
- Gait training
- Balance training
- Functional retraining
- Similar to stroke rehabilitation principles

**If Hearing Loss**
- PT coordinates with audiology
- Balance assessment (hearing loss affects vestibular system)
- Vestibular rehabilitation if needed
- Balance training for safety

**If Cognitive Impairment**
- PT adapts instructions (simplified, written)
- Safety awareness
- Functional training for ADLs
- Coordination with cognitive rehabilitation

**If Seizure Disorder Develops**
- Safety precautions during PT
- Seizure response plan
- Activity modification
- Equipment adjustments

**General PT Principles for Survivors**
- Comprehensive neurological assessment
- Address identified deficits
- Functional restoration
- Prevention of secondary complications
- Patient/family education
- Psychosocial support

---

## 6. KEY POINTS TO REMEMBER

✓ **Medical emergency** - requires immediate hospitalization
✓ **Classic triad** - fever, headache, neck stiffness
✓ **Meningeal signs** - nuchal rigidity, Kernig's, Brudzinski's
✓ **Petechial rash** - suggests meningococcal meningitis
✓ **Lumbar puncture** - confirms diagnosis
✓ **Bacterial most serious** - high mortality if untreated
✓ **Antibiotics immediately** - cannot wait for culture
✓ **Neurological sequelae** - common in survivors (hearing loss, cognitive)
✓ **PT role limited acutely** - focuses on prevention
✓ **Rehabilitation if deficits** - address motor, cognitive, sensory changes
✓ **Preventive measures** - vaccines available
✓ **Long-term follow-up** - assess for complications
