# Unit III: Muscular Dystrophy - Comprehensive Study Guide

## 1. DEFINITION & PATHOPHYSIOLOGY

### Definition
Muscular Dystrophy (MD) is a group of progressive genetic muscle disorders characterized by weakness and wasting of skeletal muscles due to defects in muscle proteins. The primary defect is in muscle fibers, leading to progressive degeneration and replacement by fat and connective tissue.

### Classification

**Duchenne Muscular Dystrophy (DMD)**
- X-linked recessive inheritance
- Most common and severe form
- Dystrophin gene mutation
- Dystrophin protein absent or severely deficient

**Becker Muscular Dystrophy (BMD)**
- X-linked recessive inheritance
- Less severe than DMD
- Dystrophin protein present but abnormal
- Slower progression

**Limb-Girdle Muscular Dystrophy (LGMD)**
- Autosomal recessive (most common) or dominant
- Multiple subtypes (>20)
- Proximal muscles primarily affected

**Facioscapulohumeral Muscular Dystrophy (FSHD)**
- Autosomal dominant inheritance
- Affects face, shoulder girdle, upper arm muscles
- Variable severity

## 2. CLINICAL FEATURES & PRESENTATION

### Duchenne Muscular Dystrophy (DMD)

**Age of Onset & Early Signs**
- Symptoms by 2-3 years of age
- Delayed motor milestones (walking by 18 months)
- Frequent falling
- Difficulty running, jumping, climbing stairs

**Progressive Weakness Pattern**
- **Proximal weakness first:** Hip and shoulder girdles
- **Progressive to distal:** Eventually affects hands and feet
- **Symmetric pattern**

**Characteristic Gait Abnormalities**
- Waddling gait (hip weakness)
- Toe walking (calf tightness)
- Lordotic posture (gluteal weakness compensation)
- Trendelenburg sign (pelvic tilt during walking)

**Functional Decline**
- Age 5-7: Can walk but with difficulty
- Age 8-10: Begins using orthoses/braces
- Age 10-12: Losing ambulation; wheelchair transition
- Wheelchair-dependent by age 10-13 years

**Pseudohypertrophy**
- Muscles appear enlarged
- Actually infiltrated with fat and connective tissue
- Paradoxically weak
- Most prominent in calves

**Gower's Sign**
- Classic sign of proximal weakness
- Child pushes up on legs with hands to rise from floor
- Indicates gluteus maximus and hip extension weakness

**Secondary Complications**
- Respiratory muscle weakness
- Cardiac involvement (dilated cardiomyopathy)
- Joint contractures and scoliosis
- Bone fragility/osteoporosis
- Pressure ulcers (if immobile)

## 3. ASSESSMENT PROCEDURES & CLINICAL TESTS

### Diagnostic Tests

**Genetic Testing**
- DNA sequencing (confirms diagnosis)
- Deletion/duplication analysis
- Gold standard for diagnosis

**Serum Creatine Kinase (CK)**
- Markedly elevated (10,000-100,000 IU/L in DMD)
- Non-specific but sensitive
- Highest in early disease

**Electromyography (EMG)**
- Myopathic pattern
- Helps differentiate from neurogenic disease

### Clinical Examination

**Manual Muscle Testing (MMT)**
- Grade 0-5 scale
- Compare proximal vs distal
- Compare right vs left (should be symmetric)

**Functional Assessment Scales**

**North Star Ambulatory Assessment (NSAA)**
- 17 items
- Designed specifically for ambulant DMD
- 0-34 points
- Highly sensitive to change

**Hammersmith Functional Motor Scale Expanded (HFMSE)**
- 20 items
- For ambulant and non-ambulant patients
- Good psychometric properties

**Timed Functional Tests**

**6-Minute Walk Test (6MWT)**
- Distance walked in 6 minutes
- Sensitive to progression

**Timed 10-Meter Walk Test**
- Walking speed
- Decline correlates with progression

**Timed Stair Climb (TSC)**
- Time to climb 4 steps
- Excellent functional measure

**Range of Motion Assessment**
- Passive ROM (PROM) - to assess contractures
- Active ROM (AROM) - for available movement
- Key areas:
  - Ankle plantarflexion (most common contracture)
  - Hip flexion
  - Knee flexion
  - Elbow flexion

## 4. MANAGEMENT PROTOCOLS

### Phase 1: Early Ambulation (Diagnosis - Early Wheelchair Use)

**Goals**
1. Maintain function and independence
2. Prolong ambulation period
3. Prevent contractures
4. Optimize strength
5. Prevent secondary complications

**Interventions**

*1. Stretching & Flexibility*
- **Frequency:** Daily
- **Duration:** 30-60 minute sessions
- **Key areas:** 
  - Ankle plantarflexors (most important - prevents foot deformity)
  - Hip flexors
  - Knee flexors
  - Shoulder muscles
- **Techniques:**
  - Sustained static stretching (30-60 seconds)
  - Heat before stretching
  - Night splinting for ankle (dorsiflexion splint)
  - Prone lying 30 minutes/day (hip extension, ankle plantarflexor stretch)

*2. Strengthening Exercises*
- **Principle:** Maintain strength while minimizing damage
- **Precautions:** Avoid eccentric exercise (muscle damage risk)
- **Type:** Concentric, low-resistance exercise
- **Specific exercises:**
  - Gentle active-assisted exercise
  - Aquatic exercise (water supports weight, reduces stress)
  - Progressive resistance (very cautious)
  - Functional movement practice

*3. Gait Training & Walking Optimization*
- **Ambulation maintenance:** Continue walking as long as possible
- **Fatigue management:** Frequent rest breaks
- **Energy conservation:** Optimize walking patterns
- **Assistive devices:**
  - Ankle-foot orthosis (AFO): Corrects foot drop, prevents plantarflexion contracture
  - Knee-ankle-foot orthosis (KAFO): As weakness progresses
  - Canes/walkers: For balance

*4. Activity Adaptation*
- **Functional activities:**
  - Rising from floor (use of furniture, hands)
  - Climbing stairs (bannister, crutches)
  - Getting up from chair (firm chair, armrests)
- **Compensatory strategies:** Teach use of trunk, upper extremities
- **Energy conservation:** Pacing activities

### Phase 2: Wheelchair Transition & Early Wheelchair Use

**Goals**
1. Safe wheelchair mobility
2. Prevent further deterioration
3. Maintain upper extremity function
4. Prevent complications (contractures, scoliosis, pressure ulcers)

**Interventions**

*1. Wheelchair Prescription & Training*
- **Wheelchair type:** Manual vs power (depends on arm strength)
- **Proper fitting:** Seat width, height, back support
- **Safety features:** Brakes, footrests
- **Training:** Proper propulsion, transfers, safety

*2. Contracture Prevention*
- **Stretching:** Continue daily program
- **Positioning:** 
  - In wheelchair: Proper support, frequent position changes
  - In bed: Alternating positions, use of rolls/pillows
- **Night splinting:** Continue ankle splints
- **ROM exercises:** Passive range of motion

*3. Upper Extremity Maintenance*
- **Strengthen remaining function:**
  - Gentle resistance exercises
  - Functional activities (reaching, grasping)
  - Aquatic exercise

*4. Scoliosis Management*
- **Monitoring:** Regular postural assessment
- **Prevention:**
  - Proper wheelchair seating with back support
  - Spinal support garments/corsets
  - Regular repositioning

*5. Pressure Ulcer Prevention*
- **Regular position changes:** Every 30 minutes
- **Pressure relief surfaces:** Cushions, mattresses
- **Skin inspection:** Daily
- **Hygiene and moisture management**

---

## 5. KEY POINTS TO REMEMBER

✓ **Contracture prevention critical** - especially ankle plantarflexors
✓ **Stretching is daily priority** - prevent secondary deformities
✓ **Prolong ambulation** - use orthoses, optimize gait
✓ **Avoid eccentric exercise** - risk of muscle damage
✓ **Monitor cardiac/respiratory** - major complications
✓ **Psychosocial support essential** - progressive, disabling disease
✓ **Family involvement crucial** - long-term management
✓ **Scoliosis development common** - monitor and prevent
✓ **Wheelchair transition planned** - not sudden
✓ **Functional assessment critical** - tracks progression
