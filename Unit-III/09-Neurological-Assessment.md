# Unit III: Neurological Assessment - Complete Guide

## SENSORY EXAMINATION

### Modalities of Sensation

**Discriminative Touch (Light Touch)**
- Assessment: Use light wisp of cotton, fingertip
- Stimulus: Gentle touch on skin
- Response: Patient identifies location of touch
- Pathway: Dorsal column-medial lemniscus (discriminative touch)
- Distribution: Map areas of hypoesthesia or anesthesia
- Pattern: Peripheral (stocking-glove) vs dermatome pattern

**Pinprick (Pain)**
- Assessment: Use sharp object (pin or needle)
- Stimulus: Gentle prick (not deep)
- Response: "Sharp" vs "dull" identification
- Pathway: Spinothalamic tract
- Compare: Sharp vs dull discrimination
- Distribution: Identify pattern (dermatome, peripheral)

**Temperature Sensation**
- Assessment: Warm and cold objects
- Stimulus: Test tube with warm/cold water
- Response: Identification of hot/cold
- Pathway: Spinothalamic tract (same as pain)
- Note: Often tested with pain sensation (similar pathways)

**Vibration Sense**
- Assessment: Tuning fork (128 Hz or 256 Hz)
- Technique: Strike tuning fork, apply to bony prominence
- Response: Perception of vibration
- Pathway: Dorsal column-medial lemniscus
- Grading: Present/diminished/absent
- Test: Toes, ankles, knees, hips, fingers, wrists, elbows, shoulders

**Proprioception (Joint Position Sense)**
- Assessment: Move joint passively
- Technique: Move finger/toe up or down
- Response: Patient states direction of movement (eyes closed)
- Pathway: Dorsal column-medial lemniscus
- Grading: Normal/diminished/absent
- Important: Test with eyes closed

**Two-Point Discrimination**
- Assessment: Two sharp points at varying distances
- Response: Patient states "one" or "two" points
- Varies by body area:
  - Fingertips: 2-3 mm
  - Palm: 8-10 mm
  - Dorsum of hand: 30-40 mm
- Pathway: Dorsal columns
- Tests cortical sensory processing

**Stereognosis (Cortical Sensory Function)**
- Assessment: Place object in hand (eyes closed)
- Response: Patient identifies object by touch
- Requires: Intact sensory pathways + cortex
- Impairment: Astereognosis indicates cortical lesion
- Objects: Coin, key, common objects

### Sensory Pattern Recognition

**Peripheral (Stocking-Glove) Distribution**
- Indicates: Peripheral neuropathy
- Pattern: Affects distal extremities
- Causes: Diabetes, alcohol, toxins, vitamin B12 deficiency

**Dermatome Distribution**
- Indicates: Spinal nerve root lesion (radiculopathy)
- Segmental pattern following spinal nerves
- Usually: Unilateral
- Causes: Herniated disc, spinal stenosis, nerve root compression

**Hemisensory Loss**
- Indicates: Brainstem or thalamic lesion
- Pattern: Entire half of body (face, arm, leg)
- Often: Contralateral to lesion
- Causes: Stroke, tumor in brainstem/thalamus

**Sensory Level**
- Indicates: Spinal cord lesion (transverse myelitis, spinal cord injury)
- Pattern: Clear demarcation with loss below level
- Bilateral and symmetric
- Important: Document at what vertebral level loss begins

**Suspended Sensory Loss**
- Indicates: Central cord lesion (syrinx, tumor)
- Pattern: Loss in Cape-like distribution (shoulders, upper arms)
- Sensation preserved below lesion
- Classic in: Syringomyelia

## MOTOR EXAMINATION

### Manual Muscle Testing (0-5 Scale)

**Grading System**
- **0:** No contraction (paralysis)
- **1:** Trace contraction visible/palpable, no movement
- **2:** Active movement with gravity eliminated
- **2+:** Initiates against gravity
- **3-:** Against gravity but cannot sustain
- **3:** Against gravity
- **3+:** Against gravity with minimal resistance
- **4-:** Against moderate resistance
- **4:** Against moderate resistance
- **4+:** Against strong resistance
- **5:** Against full/maximal resistance (normal)

### Muscle Groups to Test

**Upper Extremity**
- Shoulder abduction (deltoid)
- Shoulder adduction (pectoralis)
- Elbow flexion (biceps)
- Elbow extension (triceps)
- Wrist flexion/extension
- Finger flexion/extension
- Finger abduction/adduction (intrinsics)

**Lower Extremity**
- Hip flexion (iliopsoas)
- Hip extension (gluteus maximus)
- Hip abduction (gluteus medius)
- Hip adduction (adductors)
- Knee flexion (hamstrings)
- Knee extension (quadriceps)
- Ankle dorsiflexion (tibialis anterior)
- Ankle plantarflexion (gastrocnemius/soleus)
- Great toe flexion/extension

### Motor Pattern Recognition

**Upper Motor Neuron Pattern (Corticospinal Lesion)**
- Weakness pattern:
  - Arms: Extensors > Flexors (arm extensors relatively spared)
  - Legs: Flexors > Extensors (hip/knee flexors relatively spared)
  - Hand: Intrinsics relatively spared
- Tone: Increased (spasticity, "clasp-knife")
- Reflexes: Hyperactive/brisk
- Plantar: Upgoing Babinski
- Prognosis: Generally better potential for recovery

**Lower Motor Neuron Pattern (Anterior Horn Cell/Nerve Lesion)**
- Weakness pattern:
  - Can affect any muscle
  - Distribution follows spinal nerve/peripheral nerve
  - May be asymmetric
- Tone: Decreased (flaccid)
- Reflexes: Hypoactive/absent
- Atrophy: Prominent
- Fasciculations: May be visible
- Prognosis: Varies; often limited recovery

**Proximal vs Distal Weakness**
- **Proximal:** Hips, shoulders primarily
  - Indicates: Myopathy, proximal weakness syndromes
  - Difficulty: Rising from chair, climbing stairs, reaching overhead
- **Distal:** Hands, feet primarily
  - Indicates: Peripheral neuropathy, motor neuron disease
  - Difficulty: Fine motor tasks, walking, foot drop

## REFLEX EXAMINATION

### Deep Tendon Reflexes (0-5 Scale)

**Grading**
- **0:** Absent (areflexia)
- **+1:** Diminished (hypo-reflexic)
- **+2:** Normal (brisk, symmetric)
- **+3:** Hyperactive
- **+4:** Very hyperactive with clonus

**Reflexes to Test**
- **Biceps reflex (C5-C6):** Flex elbow, tap biceps tendon
- **Triceps reflex (C7-C8):** Elbow flexed, tap triceps tendon
- **Brachioradialis (C5-C6):** Tap radial aspect of forearm
- **Patellar reflex (L2-L4):** Knee slightly flexed, tap patellar tendon
- **Achilles reflex (S1):** Tap Achilles tendon

### Plantar Response (Babinski Sign)

**Technique**
- Stroke sole of foot from heel to toe (lateral, curving medially)
- Use blunt object (key, reflex hammer handle)

**Normal Response (Flexor)**
- Toes curl downward (plantarflex)
- Graded 0 or "negative"

**Abnormal Response (Extensor/Babinski)**
- Great toe extends (dorsiflexes)
- Other toes fan outward
- Indicates: Upper motor neuron lesion
- Graded +1 or "positive"

### Clonus
- Involuntary rhythmic muscle contraction
- Indicates: Hyperreflexia from UMN lesion
- Graded: Number of beats
- Ankle clonus most common (tap ball of foot sharply)

## CRANIAL NERVE EXAMINATION

### CN I - Olfactory (Smell)
- Assess: Sense of smell
- Test: Common scents (coffee, lemon)
- Normal: Bilaterally equal

### CN II - Optic (Vision)
- Visual acuity: Read chart
- Visual fields: Confrontation
- Pupils: PERRL (Pupils Equal, Round, Reactive to Light)
- Fundoscopy: Optic disc, vessels, retina

### CN III, IV, VI - Ocular Motor
- Eye movements: All directions (H-pattern)
- Nystagmus: Present/absent
- Pupils: Reactivity
- Ptosis: Eyelid drooping

### CN V - Trigeminal
- Sensation: Three divisions (V1, V2, V3)
- Motor: Jaw clench (masseter)
- Corneal reflex: Light touch on cornea → blink

### CN VII - Facial
- Facial symmetry: At rest
- Eye closure: Strength and symmetry
- Forehead wrinkles: Central vs peripheral involvement
- Smile: Mouth symmetry
- Upper face: Usually bilaterally innervated (central lesion: forehead spared)
- Lower face: Unilaterally innervated

### CN VIII - Vestibulocochlear
- Hearing: Whisper test, Weber, Rinne
- Balance: Gait, Romberg test

### CN IX, X - Glossopharyngeal, Vagus
- Palate: Symmetrical elevation with phonation ("Ahhh")
- Gag reflex: Touch back of pharynx
- Swallowing: Difficulty present?
- Voice: Nasal quality, hoarseness

### CN XI - Accessory
- Trapezius: Shoulder shrug against resistance
- Sternocleidomastoid: Neck rotation against resistance

### CN XII - Hypoglossal
- Tongue: Protrusion midline vs deviation
- Atrophy: Visible wasting
- Fasciculations: Involuntary contractions

## BALANCE & COORDINATION

### Static Balance
- **Standing unsupported:** Normal ability
- **Narrow base of support:** Feet close together
- **Tandem stance:** One foot in front of other
- **Single-leg stance:** Ability to stand on one leg

### Dynamic Balance
- **Functional reach:** How far can reach forward
- **Weight shifting:** Side to side, forward/backward
- **Perturbation:** Gentle push - ability to recover

### Coordination Tests
- **Finger-to-nose:** Accuracy and speed
- **Rapid alternating movements:** Palm up/down on thigh
- **Heel-shin test:** Running heel down shin
- **Diadochokinesia:** Ability to perform rapid alternating movements

### Gait Assessment
- Base of support: Width
- Step length: Symmetry
- Cadence: Rhythm and speed
- Arm swing: Symmetry and amplitude
- Posture: Alignment
- Turn: Ability and safety
- Walking stairs: Safety and effort

### Specific Tests
- **Romberg test:** Standing, feet together, eyes closed
  - Positive: Falling or severe sway with eyes closed
  - Indicates: Loss of proprioception
  
- **Tandem walking:** Walking heel-to-toe in straight line
  - Assesses: Balance and coordination

- **C4:** Shoulder tip
- **C5:** Lateral arm
- **C6:** Thumb, lateral hand
- **C7:** Middle finger
- **C8:** Medial hand, pinky
- **T1:** Medial forearm

## DERMATOMES (Lower Extremity)

- **L1:** Inguinal area
- **L2:** Anterior thigh
- **L3:** Anterior knee, medial shin
- **L4:** Medial foot
- **L5:** Dorsum of foot, lateral leg
- **S1:** Lateral foot, plantar surface
- **S2:** Posterior leg, buttock

## MYOTOMES (Major Nerve Roots)

**Upper Extremity**
- C5: Shoulder abduction, elbow flexion
- C6: Wrist extension
- C7: Elbow extension, wrist flexion
- C8-T1: Finger flexion, intrinsic hand muscles

**Lower Extremity**
- L2-L3: Hip flexion, knee extension
- L4: Knee extension, ankle dorsiflexion
- L5: Ankle dorsiflexion, toe extension, hip abduction
- S1: Ankle plantarflexion, knee flexion, hip extension

## TRANSFER & POSITIONING TECHNIQUES

### Safe Transfer Methods

**Bed to Chair Transfer (Dependent Patient)**
1. Raise head of bed to 45 degrees
2. Move patient to edge of bed
3. Support patient standing
4. Pivot to chair
5. Lower patient to sitting

**Manual Techniques**
- Use legs (not back) for lifting
- Keep load close to body
- Avoid twisting
- Use slide sheets to reduce friction
- Two-person assist for heavy patients

### Positioning Guidelines

**Supine Position**
- Pillow under head (small)
- Pillow under knees (prevents back strain)
- Pillows under ankles (prevents pressure ulcers, plantarflexion)

**Side-Lying**
- Pillow under head
- Pillow between knees
- Pillow supporting back
- Prevents pressure ulcers

**Prone Position** (If tolerated)
- Head turned to side
- Pillow under chest
- Prevents hip flexor contractures

**Sitting**
- Support back
- Feet flat on ground/footrest
- Hip/knee flexion ~90 degrees
- Prevents pressure ulcers

## KEY ASSESSMENT POINTS FOR PRACTICALS

✓ **Systematic examination** - organized, methodical approach
✓ **Compare sides** - bilateral symmetry important
✓ **Document clearly** - specific findings, not general impressions
✓ **Pattern recognition** - interpret findings (UMN vs LMN, etc.)
✓ **Identify deficits** - guide treatment planning
✓ **Safety first** - proper technique prevents injury
✓ **Patient cooperation** - clear instructions essential
✓ **Standardized testing** - use scales and formal tests
✓ **Baseline assessment** - guides progression monitoring
✓ **Functional relevance** - connect deficits to function
