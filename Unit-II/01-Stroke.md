# Unit II: Stroke - Comprehensive Study Guide

## 1. DEFINITION & PATHOPHYSIOLOGY

### Definition
Stroke, also known as cerebrovascular accident (CVA), is a sudden onset neurological deficit resulting from interruption of cerebral blood supply, lasting more than 24 hours or resulting in death. It's characterized by acute loss of cerebral function due to disturbance of the blood supply to the brain.

### Types of Stroke

#### Ischemic Stroke (85% of cases)
- **Thrombotic Stroke:** Blood clot forms in cerebral artery
- **Embolic Stroke:** Blood clot travels from heart or proximal vessel
- **Lacunar Stroke:** Small vessel disease affecting deep brain structures

#### Hemorrhagic Stroke (15% of cases)
- **Intracerebral Hemorrhage:** Bleeding within brain tissue
- **Subarachnoid Hemorrhage:** Bleeding in subarachnoid space

### Pathophysiology
- Loss of cerebral blood flow → Ischemia
- Within minutes: energy failure, loss of ion gradients
- Within hours: cell death, infarction
- Penumbra (surrounding tissue) may be salvageable early

## 2. ETIOLOGY & RISK FACTORS

### Modifiable Risk Factors
- Hypertension (most significant)
- Diabetes mellitus
- Hyperlipidemia
- Smoking
- Obesity
- Sedentary lifestyle
- Excessive alcohol consumption
- Atrial fibrillation
- Poor diet

### Non-Modifiable Risk Factors
- Age (increases with age >55 years)
- Gender (higher in males)
- Family history
- Race/Ethnicity
- Prior stroke or TIA

---

## 3. CLINICAL FEATURES & PRESENTATION

### Acute Presentation (FAST Criteria)
- **F - Face:** Facial drooping, asymmetry
- **A - Arm:** Arm weakness, drift
- **S - Speech:** Speech slurring, aphasia
- **T - Time:** Time of onset is critical

### Common Neurological Deficits

#### Motor Deficits
- Hemiplegia or hemiparesis
- Spasticity (develops over days to weeks)
- Abnormal muscle tone
- Loss of selective motor control

#### Sensory Deficits
- Hemianesthesia
- Loss of proprioception
- Sensory extinction

#### Cognitive & Language Deficits
- Aphasia (expressive, receptive, global)
- Apraxia
- Agnosia
- Neglect syndrome
- Cognitive impairment

#### Visual Deficits
- Hemianopia
- Diplopia
- Visual neglect

#### Cerebellar Signs (if cerebellar involvement)
- Ataxia
- Dysarthria
- Nystagmus

## 4. ASSESSMENT PROCEDURES & CLINICAL TESTS

### A. Clinical Examination

#### 4.1 Neurological Examination

**Consciousness & Orientation**
- Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS)
- Mini-Mental State Examination (MMSE)

**Cranial Nerves Assessment**
- CN II: Visual fields, acuity
- CN III, IV, VI: Ocular movements
- CN V: Facial sensation
- CN VII: Facial symmetry, movement
- CN IX, X: Gag reflex, voice quality
- CN XII: Tongue deviation

**Motor Assessment**

*Strength Testing (Manual Muscle Testing - MMT)*
- Grade 0-5 scale for upper and lower extremities
- Test bilateral comparison
- Focus on antigravity movements

*Tone Assessment*
- Normal tone (0)
- Slight increase (1)
- Marked increase (2-3)
- Rigid (4)
- Use Ashworth Scale or Modified Ashworth Scale

*Coordination & Cerebellar Signs*
- Finger-to-nose test
- Rapid alternating movements
- Heel-shin test
- Dysdiadochokinesia assessment

**Sensory Assessment**
- Light touch
- Pinprick
- Temperature
- Proprioception
- Vibration sense
- Two-point discrimination

**Reflex Assessment**
- Biceps, triceps, brachioradialis
- Patellar, Achilles
- Plantar response (Babinski sign)
- Hyperreflexia indicates upper motor neuron lesion

#### 4.2 Functional Assessment

**National Institute of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS)**
- 11-item scale (0-42 points)
- Assesses: consciousness, extraocular movement, visual fields, facial movement, motor strength, ataxia, sensory, language, dysarthria, extinction/inattention
- Higher score = more severe deficit

**Modified Rankin Scale (mRS)**
- 0-6 scale
- Assesses functional disability
- 0 = No symptoms
- 6 = Dead

**Barthel Index**
- Measures activities of daily living (ADL)
- 10 items
- Scoring: 0-100 (higher = better function)
- Items: feeding, bathing, grooming, dressing, bowel control, bladder control, toileting, transfers, mobility, stairs

**Fugl-Meyer Assessment (FMA)**
- 226 points maximum
- Motor: upper extremity (66), lower extremity (34), balance (14)
- Sensory: upper extremity (24), lower extremity (24)
- Joint range, pain (24)
- Excellent reliability and validity for stroke

**Berg Balance Scale (BBS)**
- 14 items
- Assesses functional balance
- Each item 0-4 points (56 total)
- High reliability and predictive value for fall risk

**Timed Up and Go (TUG)**
- Time to rise from chair, walk 3 meters, return, sit
- Normal: <12 seconds
- High fall risk: >14 seconds

**6-Minute Walk Test**
- Measures aerobic endurance
- Distance covered in 6 minutes
- Reflects community ambulation capacity

**Modified Ashworth Scale (MAS)**
- Measures spasticity
- 0-4+ scale
- 0 = No increase in tone
- 4 = Rigidity

#### 4.3 Gait Assessment
- Gait pattern analysis
- Weight distribution
- Step length symmetry
- Base of support
- Cadence and speed
- Use of assistive devices

## 5. MANAGEMENT PROTOCOLS

### A. Acute Phase (0-72 hours)

**Medical Management**
- Thrombolytic therapy (tPA within 4.5 hours)
- Antiplatelet agents (aspirin)
- Anticoagulation (if indicated)
- Blood pressure management
- Glucose control
- Temperature management

**PT Role in Acute Phase**
- Bed positioning to prevent complications
- Passive and active-assisted range of motion
- Prevent contractures
- Monitor vital signs
- Early mobilization (when medically stable)
- Assess swallowing (in coordination with SLP)

### B. Subacute Phase (1-6 weeks)

**Goals**
1. Restore motor control and strength
2. Improve functional mobility
3. Prevent secondary complications
4. Facilitate neuroplasticity

**Treatment Approaches**

**1. Motor Retraining**

*Task-Specific Training*
- Repetitive practice of functional movements
- Progressive difficulty
- High intensity and high frequency
- Examples: reaching, grasping, walking, stair climbing

*Constraint-Induced Movement Therapy (CIMT)*
- Constraint of non-affected limb
- Intensive practice of affected limb
- 2-3 weeks, 6 hours/day
- Most effective in first 3 months post-stroke

*Mirror Therapy*
- Use of mirror to reflect movements of non-affected side
- Beneficial for motor recovery
- Particularly useful for upper extremity

*Repetitive Practice*
- 1000+ repetitions per session recommended
- Promotes motor learning
- Engages neuroplasticity mechanisms

**2. Strength Training**
- Progressive resistance exercises
- Initially 1-2 sets × 8-12 repetitions
- Increase load progressively
- Focus on functional patterns
- Evidence: effective and safe, even in early stages

**3. Gait Training**

*Early Gait Training (if medically stable)*
- Parallel bars
- Body weight supported treadmill training (BWSTT)
- Overground walking
- Progressive distance and speed
- Address gait deviations:
  - Impaired dorsiflexion → foot drop
  - Circumduction gait
  - Stiff knee gait
  - Inadequate hip/knee flexion

*Advanced Gait Training*
- Outdoor walking
- Uneven surfaces
- Stairs and inclines
- Community ambulation training

**4. Balance and Coordination Training**

*Static Balance*
- Sit to stand
- Standing unsupported
- Narrow base of support

*Dynamic Balance*
- Weight shifting
- Reaching activities
- Perturbation training
- Functional activities (stepping, turning)

*Coordination Exercises*
- Finger-to-nose
- Heel-shin
- Rapid alternating movements
- In functional contexts

**5. Spasticity Management**

*Prevention*
- Optimal positioning
- Regular stretching
- Range of motion exercises
- Active movement whenever possible

*Treatment*
- Stretching programs (30-45 minutes daily)
- Heat application before stretching
- Strength training (paradoxical: reduces spasticity)
- Functional electrical stimulation (FES)
- Serial splinting/casting
- Tone-reducing techniques
  - Reciprocal inhibition
  - Slow reversal techniques
  - Contract-relax methods

*Pharmacological*
- Baclofen
- Tizanidine
- Botulinum toxin injections (focal spasticity)

**6. Proprioceptive Neuromuscular Facilitation (PNF)**
- Uses patterns of movement
- Promotes neuromuscular re-education
- Diagonal patterns
- Rhythmic stabilization for stability
- Timing for emphasis

**7. Neuroplasticity-Based Training**
- High intensity, high repetition
- Task-specific, meaningful activities
- Feedback mechanisms
- Practice during optimal windows (first 3 months critical)
- Distributed practice (multiple sessions)

### C. Chronic Phase (>6 weeks)

**Goals**
1. Maximize functional independence
2. Prevent recurrent stroke
3. Improve quality of life
4. Community reintegration

**Treatment Focus**
- Advanced functional training
- Community mobility
- Vocational/leisure activities
- Fall prevention
- Long-term exercise programs
- Home safety modifications

---

## 6. KEY POINTS TO REMEMBER

✓ Early assessment and intervention crucial
✓ Task-specific, repetitive training most effective
✓ Address both motor and cognitive deficits
✓ Involve family/caregivers early
✓ Use evidence-based outcome measures
✓ High-intensity practice promotes recovery
✓ Consider window of neuroplasticity (first 3 months optimal)
✓ Fall risk assessment and prevention critical
✓ Psychosocial support essential
