# Unit II: Parkinson's Disease - Comprehensive Study Guide

## 1. DEFINITION & PATHOPHYSIOLOGY

### Definition
Parkinson's Disease (PD) is a progressive neurodegenerative disorder characterized by progressive loss of dopamine-producing neurons in the substantia nigra pars compacta (SNpc) of the basal ganglia. It typically manifests with motor symptoms of rigidity, bradykinesia, and tremor, along with non-motor symptoms.

### Pathophysiology

**Neurobiological Changes**
- Loss of dopaminergic neurons (50-60% loss before symptom onset)
- Depletion of dopamine in striatum
- Accumulation of alpha-synuclein (Lewy bodies)
- Dysfunction of basal ganglia circuits
- Affects: substantia nigra, locus coeruleus, nucleus basalis of Meynert

**Circuit Dysfunction**
- Disruption of direct and indirect motor pathways
- Excessive inhibition of thalamus
- Impaired motor initiation and execution
- Loss of automatic movement control

## 2. ETIOLOGY & RISK FACTORS

### Risk Factors

**Non-Modifiable Factors**
- Age (>60 years, mean age 60-65 at onset)
- Gender (1.5:1 male predominance)
- Family history/Genetics

**Modifiable Factors**
- Environmental exposure (pesticides, herbicides)
- Head trauma/repeated concussions

## 3. CLINICAL FEATURES & PRESENTATION

### Motor Symptoms (Cardinal Features)

#### 1. TREMOR (70% of patients at onset)
- **Type:** Resting tremor (4-6 Hz)
- **Character:** "Pill-rolling" tremor (thumb + fingers)
- **Affected areas:** Hands initially, spreads to feet, lips, jaw
- **Features:**
  - Present at rest
  - Disappears with voluntary movement
  - Increases with stress, concentration
  - Decreases during sleep

#### 2. RIGIDITY
- **Type:** Uniform, "lead-pipe" rigidity (constant throughout movement)
- **With tremor:** "Cogwheel" rigidity (superimposed tremor)
- **Features:**
  - Present in all planes of motion
  - Increases with passive movement
  - Exacerbated by mental activity
  - Asymmetrical initially

#### 3. BRADYKINESIA (Most disabling symptom)
- **Definition:** Slowness of voluntary movement
- **Features:**
  - Slow initiation of movement
  - Slow execution of movement
  - Movement amplitude decreases with repetition (decrement)
  - Difficulty with sequential/simultaneous movements
- **Manifestations:**
  - Slow speech (hypophonia - reduced volume)
  - Micrographia (small handwriting)
  - Reduced facial expression (mask face)
  - Slow chewing and swallowing
  - Difficulty getting up from chair
  - Reduced arm swing when walking

#### 4. POSTURAL INSTABILITY
- **Onset:** Later in disease
- **Features:**
  - Loss of automatic postural reflexes
  - Impaired righting reflexes
  - Impaired balance reactions
  - Tendency to fall backward or forward
  - Stooped posture: flexed neck, trunk, hips, knees
  - Festination: involuntary acceleration of gait

### Gait Disturbances

**Parkinsonian Gait Characteristics**
- Shortened stride length
- Reduced cadence initially (later increased due to festination)
- Reduced arm swing (asymmetrical)
- Stooped posture
- Festinating gait (uncontrolled acceleration)
- Freezing of gait (sudden inability to initiate or continue walking)
- Reduced heel-strike
- Shuffling appearance

**Freezing of Gait (FOG)**
- Sudden, transient inability to move legs
- Paradoxical kinesia: Can move in response to external cues
- Triggered by: stress, crowded spaces, turning
- Can lead to falls

**Paradoxical Kinesia**
- Sudden improvement in movement during emergency/under pressure
- Patient with severe bradykinesia may suddenly run from danger
- Demonstrates brain's ability to use alternative pathways under stress

### Non-Motor Symptoms

**Cognitive & Neuropsychiatric**
- Mild cognitive impairment (early)
- Parkinson's Disease Dementia (PDD) - 24-31% of patients
- Depression (30-40% of patients)
- Anxiety disorders
- Apathy (reduced motivation)
- Sleep disturbances: REM sleep behavior disorder, insomnia

**Autonomic Dysfunction**
- Orthostatic hypotension (drop in BP on standing)
- Reduced sweating
- Seborrheic dermatitis
- Urinary urgency/frequency
- Constipation (very common)
- Sexual dysfunction

**Sensory Symptoms**
- Aching pain (musculoskeletal from rigidity)
- Restless leg syndrome
- Reduced smell (anosmia - early symptom)

**Speech & Swallowing**
- Hypophonia (soft voice) → dysarthria
- Dysphonia (hoarse voice)
- Dysphagia (swallowing difficulty) → aspiration risk
- Drooling (excessive salivation)

## 4. ASSESSMENT PROCEDURES & CLINICAL TESTS

### Unified Parkinson's Disease Rating Scale (UPDRS) - Motor Section

*Part III: Motor Examination (0-56 points)*
- Speech, Facial expression, Rigidity (all body areas)
- Finger taps, Hand movements, Rapid alternating movements
- Leg agility, Arising from chair, Posture
- Gait, Postural stability, Body bradykinesia

### Specific Tests

**Tremor Assessment**
- Amplitude (mild, moderate, severe)
- Frequency
- Distribution (unilateral vs bilateral)
- Response to voluntary movement

**Rigidity Testing**
- Passive range of motion through all joints
- At slow and fast speeds
- With mental distraction (increases rigidity)
- Documentation of "lead pipe" vs "cogwheel"

**Bradykinesia Assessment**
- **Finger tapping:** Tap thumb with index finger repeatedly
  - Normal: >4 taps/second, consistent
  - Abnormal: <4 taps/second, decrement
- **Hand pronation/supination:** Rapid movement
- **Foot tapping:** Tap floor with heel
- **Rapid alternating movements:** Slap knee with alternating surfaces
- **Drawing spiral:** Micrographia assessment
- **Gait speed:** Reduced cadence

**Postural Instability (Pull Test)**
- Patient stands, examiner gives sudden backward pull on shoulders
- Normal: Takes 1-2 steps to recover, maintains balance
- Abnormal: Takes >2 steps (stage 3), or falls (stage 4)

**Functional Assessment Scales**

**Hoehn and Yahr Staging Scale**
- **Stage 1:** Unilateral symptoms only
- **Stage 2:** Bilateral symptoms without balance problems
- **Stage 3:** Mild to moderate bilateral disease with postural instability
- **Stage 4:** Severe disability with rigidity and bradykinesia
- **Stage 5:** Confined to bed or wheelchair

**Timed Up and Go (TUG)**
- Normal PD: 12-15 seconds
- Advanced PD: >30 seconds
- High fall risk

**Berg Balance Scale (BBS)**
- 14 items
- Predicts fall risk
- Score <45 indicates high fall risk

**Six-Minute Walk Test**
- Reduced in PD due to bradykinesia and reduced stride
- Distance <350 meters indicates significant limitation

## 5. MANAGEMENT PROTOCOLS

### A. Exercise Prescription

**Frequency & Intensity**
- **Frequency:** 3-5 days per week minimum
- **Intensity:** Moderate to vigorous when tolerated
- **Duration:** 20-60 minutes per session

**Aerobic Exercise**
- Walking
- Cycling (recumbent or stationary)
- Dancing
- Aquatic exercise (reduced fall risk)
- Tai chi (excellent for balance and coordination)

**Resistance Exercise**
- 2-3 times per week
- 8-10 exercises per session
- 2-3 sets × 8-12 repetitions
- Progressive load

**Flexibility**
- Daily stretching
- Hold 30-60 seconds
- 5-10 repetitions each muscle group

**Balance Training**
- 3-5 times per week
- Varied difficulty levels
- Include proprioceptive input

### Phase 1: Mild Disease (Hoehn & Yahr 1-2)

**Goals**
1. Maintain strength and flexibility
2. Optimize movement patterns
3. Prevent falls
4. Promote independence

**Interventions**

*1. Flexibility and Range of Motion*
- Daily stretching program
- Emphasis on flexed postures:
  - Neck extensors (chin tucks)
  - Trunk extensors (prone over roll, bridging)
  - Hip extensors
  - Ankle dorsiflexors
- 30-60 second holds, 5-10 repetitions

*2. Strength Training*
- Progressive resistance exercises
- 2-3 times per week
- Upper and lower extremities
- Axial muscles (core)
- Functional patterns:
  - Squats for sit-to-stand
  - Step-ups/step-downs
  - Lunges (with balance support)
  - Arm exercises with resistance

*3. Gait Training*
- Normal walking patterns
- Conscious strategies to overcome bradykinesia:
  - "Think big" movements
  - Exaggerated arm swing
  - High stepping
  - Use of cues:
    - **Visual cues:** Lines/stripes to step over
    - **Auditory cues:** Metronome, music with steady beat
    - **Proprioceptive cues:** Verbal counting

*4. Balance Training*
- Static balance activities:
  - Standing unsupported
  - Narrow base of support
  - Tandem stance progression
- Dynamic balance:
  - Weight shifting in all directions
  - Reaching activities
  - Perturbation training (gentle pushes)
  - Step-ups
  - Sit-to-stand practice

### Phase 2: Moderate Disease (Hoehn & Yahr 2-3)

**Goals**
1. Maintain mobility and independence
2. Improve balance and reduce falls
3. Manage gait disturbances
4. Manage freezing of gait if present

**Interventions**

*1. Freezing of Gait Management (if present)*
- **Internal cues:**
  - Mental countdown: "3-2-1-go"
  - Marching action with knees high
  - Dancing (rhythmic activity)
  
- **External cues:**
  - **Visual:** Laser cane, lines on floor, tape
  - **Auditory:** Metronome, music
  - **Tactile:** Tapping on leg

*2. Gait Training (Advanced)*
- Treadmill walking with/without body weight support
- Overground walking with external cues
- Obstacle negotiation
- Turning strategies
- Backward walking (controlled)
- Stairs and inclines
- Community ambulation training

*3. Fall Prevention*
- Environmental home safety assessment
- Assistive devices (canes, proper footwear)
- Exercise for fall prevention (balance training, strength)
- Medication timing (exercises during "on" periods)

### Phase 3: Severe Disease (Hoehn & Yahr 4-5)

**Goals**
1. Maintain safety
2. Prevent complications (contractures, pressure ulcers)
3. Quality of life maintenance
4. Caregiver education and support

**Interventions**

*1. Range of Motion Programs*
- Passive ROM to prevent contractures
- 2-3 times daily
- All joints, especially flexed patterns

*2. Positioning Programs*
- Frequent position changes (hourly)
- Prevention of pressure ulcers
- Splinting to prevent contractures

*3. Caregiver Support*
- Transfer training
- Proper body mechanics
- Emotional support
- Support groups

---

## 6. KEY POINTS TO REMEMBER

✓ **Bradykinesia most disabling** - prioritize movement initiation strategies
✓ **External cueing very effective** - teach patient and family to use
✓ **Exercise delays progression** - emphasize importance
✓ **Balance training crucial** - falls are major complication
✓ **Disease is progressive** - regularly reassess and adjust
✓ **Non-motor symptoms important** - screen for depression, cognitive changes
✓ **Medication timing matters** - schedule PT during "on" times
✓ **Caregiver involvement essential** - prevent burnout and injury
✓ **Tai chi and dance excellent** - rhythmic, balance-improving modalities
✓ **Freezing of gait addressable** - multiple cueing strategies exist
